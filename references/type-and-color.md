# Type and Color

Tactics and numbers for typography, color and accessibility on screens, beyond the frontend-design skill's own rules. Open it when you pick or review a palette, a type system, or an a11y pass.

## Review passes that find the most
### Grayscale and squint every screen
Apply `filter: grayscale(1)` and blur your eyes. Text, buttons, borders and states must still separate. Two colors that turn the same gray have weak contrast whatever their hue. The primary CTA should still be the first thing you see. "If you have to squint to see the colors, it does not work."
- Look for: selected vs unselected, error vs normal, and chart series that merge in gray.
- Fix: change lightness (value) first, then hue. Add an icon or label where gray hides the state.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([How To Choose The RIGHT Logo Color](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-Zw7CbM-ps)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber LOGO Designs! Ep 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fx0etc14nk)); SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design Principles Master Class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HonJiqr7eqY))

### Build a contrast matrix for the tokens
List every text and surface token against every other, mark pass or fail, and allow only passing pairs in components. Fix the token, not the single element. Test both light and dark text on each colored surface: light text can pass where dark fails. On a green theme, dark text missed AA, so text went white and the CTA went black.
- Look for: muted gray text, badges, placeholder, disabled-looking footers, text on tinted cards.
- Fix: one matrix in the design tokens doc. Run Chrome DevTools CSS Overview (More tools, CSS Overview, Capture) on your own build to list low-contrast text, stray colors, fonts and sizes in one report.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Best practices to build Accessible Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fHkr3SL51Y)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS Pt. 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vBuyR8uF380)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([9 Web Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeMtV8BaZt0)); willpatersondesign ([Design Mistakes You NEED to Avoid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXrNNVOgEd0))

### Judge the palette on a real page, in several versions
Drop the hex codes into a live-looking page, not a swatch grid. Duplicate the layout 4 or 5 times in different palettes (beige, full brand color, white, green, inverted dark), driven by CSS variables, screenshot each, then pick. Also compare a light-dominant and a dark-dominant version. The same color looks different beside different neighbors, so test the brand color on every real background (white, dark, tinted card).
- Look for: a CTA that was loud on the swatch but blends into its section, a hue that goes muddy on dark.
Sources: TimGabe ([9 SECRETS Pt. 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vBuyR8uF380)); willpatersondesign ([5 FREE Color Tools For Graphic Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0dQsITO7ApQ)); SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design FULL Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nKoNZ1u21ss)); SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design Principles Master Class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HonJiqr7eqY))

### Screenshot five competitors before choosing the accent
List their dominant hues, then decide on purpose to fit the category or break it. Snapchat's yellow stands out among blue social logos. If every rival is blue and white, test a distinct accent that keeps legibility and trust cues. Also ask what the palette says at first glance (the category, the mood) and swap in two alternative accents to see if the tone shifts. Avoid default SaaS blue unless you want to look like everyone.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Advanced Colour Selection Techniques](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HTFAAef1Y8k)); willpatersondesign ([Redesigning Your Logos Ep8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-bwYtTjcjqc)); willpatersondesign ([How To Choose The RIGHT Logo Color](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-Zw7CbM-ps))

### Judge display type by blur, flip and stepping back
At hero size, blur your eyes or flip the headline upside down so meaning drops away and only gaps show. Step back to judge leading and tracking. Check awkward pairs (AV, To, RA, GI, round letters beside straight ones) and look for holes or dark patches. Check type at real device size and zoomed out, not only zoomed in.
- Fix: nudge `letter-spacing` or wrap a pair in a span, for headlines and wordmarks only. Keep body on the font's own kerning.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The BEST Way To Understand Tracking & Kerning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vo34Vp6_vkA)); willpatersondesign ([Stop Kerning Wrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XuVBc8h31Fo)); Will Paterson Design ([5 MIND BLOWING Typography Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y2Tn9DS4ROs)); willpatersondesign ([Design Mistakes You NEED to Avoid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXrNNVOgEd0))

## Color roles and the accent budget
### Give every color one job and audit it
Map roles instead of choosing by taste: action (buttons, CTAs), ink (headlines, key text, needs contrast), support (guides flow between sections), neutral (backgrounds), anchor (borders, dividers, subtle icons), plus success, warning and error. Name variables by role (`--action`, `--text-strong`, `--border`, `--danger`). One action means one color everywhere: a header button in a different color than the hero button is an inconsistency. Repeat the accent in a few small places (logo mark, active tab, key link) to tie the page together.
- Look for: the action color used on decorative items, a second or third accent on buttons.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The NEW 2025 Color System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SLHv_sEHc9c)); DesignSpo ([How to Make Your Website Look PROFESSIONAL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtjOJkmnzs8)); TimGabe ([Design Better Than 99% of UI Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E)); SatoriGraphics ([5 Pro Techniques You're FORGETTING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bsbFFRU5eck))

### Treat 60/30/10 as a pixel budget for the accent
About 60% neutral surfaces, 30% text, cards or brand, 10% accent. All sources call it a guide, not a law, and they split the 30% differently (neutral vs brand), so just hold the accent to about 10% or less. A Nike-style reference may use only about 5% accent. More than one or two accent hues on a screen is a smell. A beginner screen that was mostly strong red felt stressful and nothing stood out. Luxury looks cheap when the accent is overused. When the two main colors are similar, 50/30/10/10 with two accents works.
- Look for: accent covering much more than a tenth of the screen, brand color on every card.
- Fix: move most brand-color areas to neutrals or a 5 to 8% tint; keep saturation for the CTA and active state.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video You Need To Improve Your Graphic Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VA5QmkoQqz0)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video That Will Make You A Better Designer In ONE DAY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exYfWADm-Ns)); TimGabe ([4 levels of UI/UX design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86PGRyQjdzQ)); SatoriGraphics ([4 Smart Ways To INSTANTLY Improve Your Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szOxvwj8XlI))

### Make the CTA differ from its surroundings in two ways
The hue matters less than being glaringly different from its neighbors. SatoriGraphics cites: a HubSpot test where a red button beat a green one by 21% in click-through, and says contrast with the surroundings is the lesson, not red. A blue button on a pink-heavy page beats another pink one. Pick the one hue not used elsewhere on that surface, then differ in a second axis too (weight or size). On a cool or neutral UI a warm accent gives one focal point; keep it to one warm focal point per view. On a colored section, switch the CTA to a dark or opposite tone so it contrasts with the section.
- Look for: outline-only primary buttons (people do not see or click them), CTA the same hue as the hero art.
- Fix: ghost or outline only for tertiary actions. A secondary button uses the primary color at about 5% fill plus a 5% border so it does not compete.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Colour Theory Video You Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKtTNOgfhAU)); SatoriGraphics ([5 Pro Techniques You're FORGETTING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bsbFFRU5eck)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Give Me 7 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NTKwpAVcHg)); TimGabe ([Design Better Than 99% of UI Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E))

### Pace color down the page and in charts
Do not spread color everywhere. The eye lands on accent focal points, glides to muted support, rests in a large neutral section, and ends on the final CTA in the action color. A grayscale layout with one colored element pulls the eye to it. In charts, use gray for context and one accent for the thing that matters (a red dot on gray bars tells the user where to look). Cut glow and blur effects that carry no meaning.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The NEW 2025 Color System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SLHv_sEHc9c)); SatoriGraphics ([LESS Than 10% Of Designers Know This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGYCn9qYjUQ)); TimGabe ([4 levels of UI/UX design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86PGRyQjdzQ))

### Keep navigation and chrome neutral
Sidebar and tab bar stay white, gray or dark. Do not give each tab its own color or mix many colors between top and bottom bars. The accent marks the active state and the primary CTA. Dim inactive items with opacity but keep at least 3:1 against the bar.
Sources: uxpeak ([Top UI/UX Design Tips - Bottom Mobile Navigation Bar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLJ40GV2XEc)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS Pt. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cmSBbnrFGPg))

## Building a palette
### Start from feeling words, audience and product, not taste
Write 3 feeling words and 3 personality words, then map them to base, background and accent roles. Example: soft blue base for calm and secure, pastel backgrounds, one orange accent only on CTAs. Color meaning is contextual, not "blue builds trust": red plus yellow reads fast food, red plus green reads Christmas, and meaning shifts by culture (red is luck in China, danger in the West). Associations must be real: a product that looks natural but is not creates distrust. SatoriGraphics cites ColorCom research: people judge a product within 90 seconds and 62 to 90% of that is based on color. TimGabe cites: people make trust decisions about an app within 50 ms.
- Look for: "I like it" as the only reason for a hue, a playful palette on a billing or security screen.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([BEST Colour Scheme Selection](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GrV3qVjd5Ek)); DesignSpo ([The ULTIMATE Color Theory Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tKCORDK0IZU)); SatoriGraphics ([How To Choose The CORRECT COLOUR](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZDddX7c9SHI)); TimGabe ([How Addictive Apps Are Made](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw))

### Tune saturation and lightness before hue
Hue, saturation and value are three dials. Low saturation with mid values reads calm or premium. High saturation reads energetic, young, sometimes cheap. A deep burgundy and a bright cherry share a hue and feel different. If a pair clashes (purple with yellow), lower saturation or shift brightness instead of changing hue. Deeper tones feel richer than pure primaries: forest for bright green, burgundy for red, deep navy for default blue. Large surfaces stay muted, small areas carry saturation. Pastel everywhere is forgettable, so keep one saturated primary. Muddy combos (mustard with wine tones) read cheap, so view swatches together.
- Fix for calm products (insurance, health, finance): low saturation and low-contrast chrome, but CTAs and small text still hit the contrast ratio.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design Principles Master Class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HonJiqr7eqY)); SatoriGraphics ([Elevate Your Graphic Designs With These Simple Color Fixes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9Kl3rMAfFI)); SatoriGraphics ([ADVANCED Colour Theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XNkV6m4fosw)); willpatersondesign ([Pro Logo Designer VS Fiverr Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TKdsKBd7Eo8))

### Derive the whole palette from one hue
Work in HSL. For an accent, rotate hue only and keep S and L (DesignSpo says this works about 85% of the time; if not, go monochrome). A quick complement is H plus or minus 180, then tune S and L by hand because hue will be right and lightness will not. Build a 50 to 900 ramp for tints (surfaces) and shades (text). Make hover, subtle fills and borders from the brand token with alpha or `color-mix` instead of inventing new hues. Lock one color in a generator and regenerate around it, or mosaic a moodboard photo into color blocks.
Sources: DesignSpo ([20 Graphic Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGPvdevwM8o)); willpatersondesign ([How To Choose The RIGHT Logo Color](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-Zw7CbM-ps)); TimGabe ([How To Design a $5k Website](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hCKdIwbhS-s)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Only 5 Web Design Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vbFn0C-pvis))

### Choose the harmony by how much pop you need
Monochrome and analogous are calm but nothing pops, so use them for dashboard chrome. Complementary grabs attention and tires the eye, so use it for one accent and soften with tints and shades. Triadic suits charts but threatens text legibility. Split-complementary is hard to balance. Analogous with a gentle accent suits calm tools, complementary suits conversion pages.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Colour Theory Video You Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKtTNOgfhAU)); SatoriGraphics ([Master COLOUR GROUPS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r7CFyVtme9o)); DesignSpo ([The ULTIMATE Color Theory Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tKCORDK0IZU))

### Pull the accent and the photos from one source
Eyedrop the accent from the hero image and reuse it for the CTA, link hover and one highlighted element, so it appears in at least two roles. Keep photos inside the palette range with one consistent grade, as if from one shoot (a neon pink hoodie on a clean black and white site looks off). Dark overlays do not rescue mismatched category tiles: use one soft tinted background per tile with images in one style. Do not paint an object and its background the same color: card on page, icon on tile and chip on card should each differ in tone.
Sources: TimGabe ([4 Easy Tips For Stunning Landing Pages](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cocnZubvfFE)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U)); uxpeak ([Top 5 Advanced UX/UI Tips Part 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xzh8xjimmp8)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing CLEVER Subscriber DESIGN work Ep 31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uduImkvMsKU))

## Contrast and legibility on real surfaces
### Value contrast beats hue contrast
Pale yellow on white is unreadable whatever the hue; dark navy on cream is clear. Saturated complements touching shimmer and strain the eye (edge vibration): no saturated red text on blue or green. Do not set a whole text block in one accent color: choose the one hook phrase and style it, keep long text neutral. Bright orange text on saturated blue clashes; sample a darker tone from the image instead. Split-color text loses its low-contrast half when zoomed out, so each half must pass.
- Look for: white text on bright blue or a bright strip, accent-colored paragraphs, colored text on colored cards.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design Principles Master Class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HonJiqr7eqY)); SatoriGraphics ([Making FAMOUS Graphic Designs Better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tm25IxJQPWM)); SatoriGraphics ([Using Colour To Become A BETTER Designer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IzED6Q59DAY)); willpatersondesign ([Subscriber LOGO Critique](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VQlbOmF33us))

### Text over images gets a solid scrim and a worst-case check
Put a semi-transparent shape behind the text, such as `bg-black/50`, and check contrast on the busiest region of the final render, not in the design tool. If white text fails on a given image, flip that line to dark. Never put text directly on moving imagery. A dark overlay alone does not fix photos that vary in brightness.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Create Stunning Designs FAST](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKDK5QFheSQ)); SatoriGraphics ([EVERYTHING You Need To Know About Contrast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nVZ_zihVSS8)); SatoriGraphics ([Making A Spotify Design Better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLfShrWm1Ag)); hatchconference ([Piccia Neri: The EAA is coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUN5fun2T0I))

### Build text hierarchy from stepped ink strength
Use one ink at tiers: headings 100%, body about 75 to 80%, tertiary and captions about 50 to 60%. Material cites about 87% for high emphasis and about 60% for medium. Name tokens `--text-1`, `--text-2`, `--text-3`. Under a bold headline, a slightly lower-contrast, looser-leaded paragraph reads calmer. Re-measure every opacity-derived color, because 60% often fails 4.5:1.
- Look for: three gray text levels that all pass, or one that quietly fails on a tinted card.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([6 EASY Tips to 10x Any Site's Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbhLsV-Dyho)); TimGabe ([Learn Framer For Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wgMgje60Ovw)); TimGabe ([Design Better Than 99% of UI Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E)); uxpeak ([UX/UI Design Tutorial in Figma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SIoOS6tFw1Y))

### Placeholder reads as empty, and still passes
Empty inputs show lighter text than entered text so it is clear they still need filling, but placeholder must still meet contrast and never replaces a label. Same for disabled-looking footers and secondary gray text.
Sources: DesignSpo ([I Redesigned YOUR Websites Ep. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oO8MzIVyiv0)); amirmoradiUX ([Product Designer in the AI Era](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ky_F3jdnT4Y))

## Dark mode and neutrals
### Tint neutrals and text with the brand hue
Text color matters more than background. Keep the background's hue in the text. On light backgrounds lower lightness and raise saturation, on dark backgrounds raise lightness and lower saturation (Material approach). Example tokens: `hsl(220 30% 12%)` text on light, `hsl(220 15% 92%)` text on dark. Dark mode backgrounds are not pure `#000` either. A colorful brand gets one dark desaturated anchor (Slack's wordmark, eBay's darkest letter) so the eye has a resting point. Shadows take the surface hue (a darker purple on purple), never gray on a colored background, and are large-blur and low-opacity, for example `0 8px 24px rgb(... / 0.08)`.
- Look for: gray shadows that look dirty on tinted cards, cold gray text on a warm surface.
Sources: DesignSpo ([20 Graphic Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGPvdevwM8o)); uxpeak ([Top 5 UX/UI Tips Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8pMUkEbAM7g)); willpatersondesign ([10 MIND BLOWING Logo Design Tips 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gW5IpIUmOr8)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing STRANGE Subscriber LOGO Designs Ep 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c6mJJvaE0WY))

### Light on dark looks heavier, so thin it
White on dark glows and reads slightly larger and bolder than the same shape black on white. In dark mode drop weight a notch (for example 400 to about 350 on a variable font), thin icon strokes a little, and compare light and dark side by side. Ship a dedicated dark logo asset instead of `filter: invert(1)`. Check each brand color on light, dark and small mobile screens: a teal that works on desktop can blend into the background on mobile. DesignSpo notes dark sites fit developer tools because they evoke workstations; for other audiences light is fine.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([10 MIND BLOWING Logo Design Tips 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gza5-1e0IY4)); willpatersondesign ([10 MIND BLOWING Logo Design Tips 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gW5IpIUmOr8)); SatoriGraphics ([The NEW 2025 Color System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SLHv_sEHc9c)); DesignSpo ([The ULTIMATE Color Theory Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tKCORDK0IZU))

## Type system
### Derive every size from the body size and one ratio
Pick body (16px, or 18 to 20 for long reading; Medium uses 20px), then a ratio (1.25 major third is the common one; 1.2 to 1.5 and 1.618 all work) and generate steps up and down. Round values, use rem so browser font settings and zoom work, and ban off-scale sizes. Which ratio matters less than sticking to it; nudge one step by eye if it looks off. Rough targets: H1 about 180 to 200% of body, H2 130 to 150%. For UI, aim for 4 sizes and 2 weights, and let weight and color carry the rest. Define roles once (heading, body, button, label) with size, weight, line height and tracking.
- Look for: headings and body within one step of each other, one-off sizes in components.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Only 5 Web Design Skills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vbFn0C-pvis)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS Pt. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cmSBbnrFGPg)); willpatersondesign ([3 PROVEN Typography Methods](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFUXBlnTS5Y)); SatoriGraphics ([6 Typography Golden Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=za8ERlMchUE))

### Scale only headings across breakpoints
Step H1 to H3 down one size per breakpoint, or use `clamp(2rem, 7vw, 6rem)` for a hero title. Anything 18px or below keeps the same size on every device, so hierarchy survives and text never shrinks below 16. Keep body-small smaller than body-medium. Condensed display faces can run larger, and long product or plan names suit a condensed face better than a smaller size.
Sources: TimGabe ([Learn Framer For Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wgMgje60Ovw)); DesignSpo ([24 Web Design Concepts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a_6s4AKQ2VI)); DesignSpo ([How To Easily Design Pixel Perfect Websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bDC6JM0sTI)); ShantanuKumar ([How to make Design System in Notion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=El_0xWpu4Hg))

### Build hierarchy from weight and ink before size
Make weight steps two levels apart: heading 700 with body 400, not 600 with 500. Big text bold and small text light, buttons a little heavier than body (500 to 600). A thin big heading and a bold paragraph are both hard to read. Use 800 to 900 only for a few words or key numbers, and 200 to 300 only large with high contrast. Pick one bold weight and use it for every card heading. Titles semibold, subtitles regular in gray. In body copy, lift 2 or 3 keywords (weight 600 or accent ink) for rhythm. In pricing, bold and color the price, mute the billing period.
- Look for: hierarchy that relies on size alone, three bold-ish weights in one view, ultra-light hero text that looks generic.
Sources: DesignSpo ([20 Graphic Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGPvdevwM8o)); Will Paterson Design ([Great Tips to IMPROVE Your Design Work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=usv5PWBr56c)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video That Shows You How Expert Designers Think](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=brzjHzzxD0E)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing UNREAL Subscriber LOGO Designs Ep 25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jMOW2fCuNVU))

### Set tracking, leading and measure by role
Tracking: display -1% to -3% (-0.01 to -0.03em), body 0 (designers tuned it), small text +1% to +2%, uppercase labels 0.04 to 0.1em, buttons +0.01 to 0.05em. Tight reads as power and urgency, loose as calm or luxury. Never negative tracking on body. Tracking is font dependent, so re-check after swapping fonts. Leading shrinks as size grows: display 95 to 120%, body 140 to 160%. Cap prose and hero subtext near 600px (about 60ch) even on wide layouts, headlines up to about 800px. TimGabe cites a UX institute study (likely Baymard): long lines feel intimidating, users skip, understand the benefits less and convert less.
Sources: DesignSpo ([The ULTIMATE Guide To Typography For Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AXpxZMRM1EY)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([9 Web Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeMtV8BaZt0)); TimGabe ([6 UI Hacks I Wish I Knew](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=88XxC0_zs74)); SatoriGraphics ([The BEST Way To Understand Tracking & Kerning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vo34Vp6_vkA))

### Left-align long text and never mix alignments
Anything over 3 lines is left-aligned because centered lines force the eye to find each new start. Centered hero heading plus short subline is fine. Never pair a centered heading with a left-aligned body or the reverse. Turn hyphenation off on headings and short columns (`hyphens: manual`), and use `text-wrap: pretty` for clean rag.
Sources: TimGabe ([6 UI Hacks I Wish I Knew](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=88XxC0_zs74)); SatoriGraphics ([6 Typography Golden Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=za8ERlMchUE)); SatoriGraphics ([6 Tips To Improve Your Typography](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bw5HEh1uSjc))

### Keep numbers, truncation and inline items honest
Right-align number columns. A monospace or tabular face on big changing numbers (balances, rewards) stops layout shift. When truncating, keep the discriminating part: middle-truncate file paths, unit numbers and ID suffixes, with full text on focus or tooltip. A cut-off courier address that hid the apartment number is the failure case. Check inline icons, ampersands and badges sit on the text baseline. A round icon next to square ones should be about 2 to 5% larger to look equal.
Sources: amirmoradiUX ([Product Designer in the AI Era](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ky_F3jdnT4Y)); TimGabe ([4 levels of UI/UX design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86PGRyQjdzQ)); willpatersondesign ([Stop Kerning Wrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XuVBc8h31Fo)); Will Paterson Design ([5 MIND BLOWING Typography Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y2Tn9DS4ROs))

## Pairing and personality
### Pair by shared world plus clear contrast, or stay in one family
Three levels: one font; a superfamily designed together (Source Sans, Serif and Code; Roboto family; DM Sans with DM Serif); families from different designers. At level three pick the heading face first because it sets personality, then a companion with different width and structure. Avoid near-twins (Georgia with Times) that look accidental. A characterful display face over a plain neutral body reads utilitarian and trustworthy. Pairs speakers name: Archivo Black with Aleo, Oswald with Merriweather, Playfair Display with Fauna One, Cinzel with Lora. Mixed families with very different size and spacing make the eye bounce. SelfMadeWebDesigner cites: Vignelli built a career on about six typefaces and called font proliferation visual pollution.
- Look for: nav in a different family from the hero, inputs and buttons that fall back to system font.
- Fix: `font: inherit` on `button, input, select, textarea`; count `font-family` values in the build.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([6 EASY Tips to 10x Any Site's Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbhLsV-Dyho)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([9 Web Design Hacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeMtV8BaZt0)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Typography Video Graphic Designers Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a4Pj9MAPtuo)); DesignSpo ([I Redesigned YOUR Websites Ep. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oO8MzIVyiv0))

### Pick type from three tone adjectives, then check function
Write 3 adjectives for the brand voice first. Serif reads heritage and authority, rounded sans friendly, heavy block stable, and mono belongs to code and IDs (poor for long reading). A case study for a consulting firm found a complex font cheapened it, a serif looked too premium, a bold display looked like a game company, and a neutral grotesque read trustworthy. Techy or sci-fi display faces look cheap on SaaS. Then test in the real layout, not a specimen. Match the corner language: rounded type with rounded buttons, do not mix rounded and sharp corners at random. Formal products get tidier alignment, so keep billing and settings strict and save expressive type for the marketing hero. Do not pick a font because it is trending.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Typography in Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NFclZzR0Ls)); willpatersondesign ([Logo Design Process: Creating a REAL Client's Logo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Umin1iXhsn0)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Typography Video Graphic Designers Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a4Pj9MAPtuo)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Flying Nickel LOGOs Ep 27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7PPtOJC6J5E))

### Use display faces only for short, large text
Display type is for text you see rather than read, such as a headline or logo. Anything over roughly 7 to 10 words favors a readable face. Deliberately hard-to-read expressive type is fine for low-priority decoration because people read big bold things first, but never for content users must read or act on. Check licenses before shipping a webfont. Use variable fonts (`wght`, `wdth`) to tune weight and width per breakpoint, and pick a condensed family instead of `transform: scaleX`.
Sources: DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U)); SatoriGraphics ([Perfect Typographic Logos Using The GOLDEN RULES](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HEud-MS7sn4)); SatoriGraphics ([6 Graphic Design Rules You Should NEVER Break](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pkGxGoVuljY))

## Accessibility beyond the floor
### Run the six common failures, then do manual passes
Hatch Conference cites the WebAIM Million and its own audits: low contrast, missing alt, empty links and buttons, missing semantic headings, missing keyboard navigation, dynamic content not announced. A speaker at the same conference cites that automated tools catch only about 30% of issues. After axe or Lighthouse, do: keyboard through every flow, a screen reader through signup and checkout, 200% zoom, reduced motion. Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Do not report "accessible" from a scanner score.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Best practices to build Accessible Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fHkr3SL51Y)); hatchconference ([Piccia Neri: The EAA is coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUN5fun2T0I)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U))

### Give controls names that make sense out of context
Icon-only buttons get `aria-label`. In lists with repeated "Edit", "Delete" or "Add", append the item name with visually hidden text. Alt text fails three ways: it repeats the visible caption, is missing, or the image is hidden. Use `alt=""` only for decorative images or images inside a link whose text already names the target. For dense cards and table rows (a transit result), give the container one composed label: "from 20:40 until 20:57, from A to B, line S12, 17 minutes, on time".
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Best practices to build Accessible Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fHkr3SL51Y)); TimGabe ([From Figma to Framer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLWXztyA80Q)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U))

### Announce changes and move focus on purpose
Add `aria-live="polite"` for result counts, cart or ticket totals ("5 tickets selected, total ..."), toasts and form success. When the focused control disappears (dialog closes, item deleted, upload button replaced by a file row), set focus to the next logical element, for example the remove button on the uploaded file. Test by tabbing the flow and watching for resets to the top of the page.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Best practices to build Accessible Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fHkr3SL51Y))

### Test date pickers and autoplay as top failures
Cited: a blind tester said most flight booking systems lack an accessible date picker, so he left for another airline. Tab through any date control with keyboard only and VoiceOver or NVDA, and keep a typed input with a format hint beside the calendar. No autoplay motion video unless muted, with a pause control that is the first focusable item and works with Enter and Space. Auto-rotating carousels get a pause button and must not steal focus.
Sources: hatchconference ([Piccia Neri: The EAA is coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUN5fun2T0I))

### Add a second channel and a calm option
Color is the most overloaded channel. Pair status color with icon and text, which also covers cultures where red means luck, not error. Optional haptics (`navigator.vibrate`) or sound for key confirmations, always with a mute setting. Natalia Filvarova cites that multi-sensory input improves understanding and memory. Offer a calm or low-density setting for people overwhelmed by pop-ups, motion and discounts, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-color-scheme`. Design for situational limits too: sun glare, one hand, noisy train.
Sources: Natalia Filvarova ([Embrace Multisensory & Accessible Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OjlSIq29YzM)); hatchconference ([Piccia Neri: The EAA is coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUN5fun2T0I)); SatoriGraphics ([What Does Colour Mean Within Logo Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-kM6pox0OM))

### Annotate a11y at design time and publish a statement
Write accessible names, heading levels, focus order, alt text and live-region messages in the ticket before build. Start small: button labels, then headings, then alt text and icons, then hidden elements, then keyboard and screen-reader-only content. Add one a11y line to the PR checklist. Put contrast, focus states, target size and labels in the first component build, not a late audit. Publish an `/accessibility` page linked in the footer with: standard targeted (WCAG 2.2 AA), date of the latest test, known issues, remediation plan, how to report a problem. A speaker cites this frames reports as collaboration, not litigation. For the business case, the speaker cites: 65% of disabled people and their families have left a business over poor accessibility; the EU European Accessibility Act covers e-commerce and booking from June 2025, with B2C micro-enterprises (under 10 staff and under 2M euro turnover) exempt.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Best practices to build Accessible Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fHkr3SL51Y)); hatchconference ([Piccia Neri: The EAA is coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUN5fun2T0I)); Natalia Filvarova ([Embrace Multisensory & Accessible Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OjlSIq29YzM))

## Where designers disagree
- Inter-like neutral sans: DesignSpo, Will Paterson and TimGabe pick Inter, Helvetica or Montserrat as safe, trustworthy UI faces. frontend-design bans Inter and Roboto, and SelfMadeWebDesigner also says overused free fonts blend in (try Fontshare or uncut.wtf). Common ground: a neutral grotesque, but a less default one.
- Gradients: SelfMadeWebDesigner, TimGabe, SatoriGraphics and willpatersondesign use low-opacity corner gradients, a blurred copy of the hero image as a glow, vignette plus noise, a gradient mask behind text, or a 10 to 20 degree hue-shift gradient on a button. frontend-design bans gradients of any kind. TimGabe's "senior" fix also prefers flat colors over gradients.
- One emphasized headline word: DesignSpo and SatoriGraphics recommend a color, color block, underline, italic or bold-vs-light mix on one headline word (bold first words 700, rest 300). frontend-design bans accenting a single word in a headline.
- All-caps eyebrow above the headline: DesignSpo's hero pattern is eyebrow, headline, subtitle, CTA, with wide-tracked small caps. frontend-design bans ALL-CAPS eyebrows sprayed on pages. If you do use caps labels, track them 0.04 to 0.1em.
- Accent budget: the 60/30/10 sources let brand color take about 30% of a screen, and allow complementary accents or two accents. frontend-design keeps one accent and mostly monochrome. A warm, saturated CTA on a cool UI (DesignSpo, SatoriGraphics) also sits against frontend-design's under-80% saturation cap.
- Line height and size: the sources put body at 120 to 145% line height (Spotify about 1.25), 15 to 25px body, and display headings at 80 to 100% leading. frontend-design sets body 1.5 to 1.6, 16px, and headings about 1.1.
- Dark default for tech: DesignSpo says dark sites suit developer products. frontend-design lists a near-black canvas with one acid accent as a default cluster to avoid.
