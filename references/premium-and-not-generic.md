# Premium feel, escaping the generic look, consistency and polish

Open this when a screen looks "fine but forgettable", when generated UI needs to stop looking like every other SaaS template, when many screens must feel like one product, or when you are doing a final polish pass on motion, details and speed.

## Tests that expose a generic design
### Run the swap-the-logo test
Screenshot 10 to 20 competitor pages, cover every logo, drop yours in the grid. If it could swap places unnoticed, it is designed for comfort and safety. Ask what every competitor does and where the empty visual space is. Then change one defining trait (layout, type style, color, illustration approach) and keep it legible.
- Look for: a page that could be re-skinned for a gym, a restaurant or a rival by changing the logo and photos.
- Fix: pick one signature device tied to the product's domain (Polaroid-style frames for a wedding photographer, a timeline of colored time blocks for a planner) and rewrite generic CTAs to domain ones ("View your portfolio", not "Read more").
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([4 Advanced Logo Design Tips EVERY Designer Needs To Know! (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0meysRqp9TU)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber LOGO Designs! Ep 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fx0etc14nk)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design (With Examples)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA))

### Name the decisions, or add one
For each section, list the deliberate decisions in it (subject, light, type tone, spacing, copy). A page that looks sharp and technically solid but could be made in 30 seconds feels like a template. If you can only name defaults, add one specific, brief-driven choice. In review, write a one-line reason for every notable choice; if you cannot, change it.
- Look for: everything centered, the same gap everywhere, the same card style, clean symmetric layouts that read like "toast from a toaster".
- Fix: vary rhythm, scale and space where attention should go. Micro-spacing, hierarchy and contrast are levers, not recipe ingredients.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([How To Train Your Graphic Design Eye To Beat AI in 2026!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-KIWKgxu8cw)); SatoriGraphics ([How A Graphic Designer MUST Think in 2026 To Survive!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFkGbyH24AY)); TimGabe ([Do Companies Really Need Designers Anymore?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk))

### Read only the section headlines, then cover the visuals
Two checks. (1) Read section headlines top to bottom: they should read as a story (problem, stakes, turning point, product, proof, action), not a pile of feature cards. (2) Cover the visuals and read hero headline and sub: a stranger should say what the product does and for whom. One AI page "looked really innovative but I don't really know what it is for" and scored lower on UX.
- Fix: rewrite copy before polishing style. Write each section as the next beat of the story.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Let's See Who's Better...Human or AI Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5LW_-J9q9h8))

### Flag repeated grids
In a blind test of four AI site builders, the judge scored AI pages 4 to 6 on originality. Two of them were near identical: clean hero, bold title, clear CTA, then three-column feature grids repeated section after section ("like a template", "sterile"). The human page scored 31.5/40 against 6.5 to 26 for the AI pages (as the speaker stated).
- Look for: 3 or more sections sharing the same grid; identical mockup or visual for every item in a feature list or portfolio.
- Fix: alternate image-left and image-right every other section, change background or density at intervals, use a horizontal scroller or a full-bleed band once. Give each feature its own visual (live UI snippet, bespoke illustration) instead of identical cards.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Let's See Who's Better...Human or AI Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5LW_-J9q9h8)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Layouts (With Examples)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0Pk3OVbxRU))

### Ban the category cliche before you design
First answers are the category symbol: mountain for outdoors, leaf for eco, blue hexagons for blockchain, rocket for a rocket company, coffee beans for a cafe, blue humanoid robots and glowing brains for AI. In one logo contest about 25 percent of 300 entries used a film reel for a film brand. Write the 5 most obvious ideas and ban them. Also ban what every competitor uses on at least one axis (color, type style, illustration).
- Fix: borrow a visual language from a different category (a coffee brand borrowed luxury fashion mascots), or find the connection through a related, unexpected concept. For AI features, show the real interface or output instead of robot art. Do not stamp sparkle icons or "AI" labels on features; name them by what they do ("Summarize").
Sources: ShantanuKumar ([if I were to design a coffee shop... this is how I would do it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7tqu-x0dDeQ)); SatoriGraphics ([LOGO CRITIQUE: I Think This Holds Designers Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bILUZjZq4zI)); hatchconference ([Nadia Piet: What can Designers do FOR A.I.?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lzja_NoTflc)); Panel ([AI is Redesigning Design Tools - with Lovable, V0 and Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rrt_MDrpraU))

### Check the "clean but generic" trap after simplifying
A removed detail can make a mark generic: a sportswear mark that lost its crossbar felt clean but anonymous. After you strip a design, name the one feature that is unmistakably this product (a shape, a color, a motion). If you cannot, add one signature element and reuse it on every screen. Reviewers also said marks "look like a sports logo" or "an insurance ad" when they used category-typical shapes and colors.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber LOGO Designs! Ep 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fx0etc14nk)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing Your Designs On Reddit! Ep2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5k9Vj8sKMG8))

### Do not illustrate the product literally
A rocket in a rocket company's mark, a drop for a brand named Solar Drop, a plane for travel. Use a concept that conveys a feeling instead. For empty states and hero art, one clever metaphor with a small twist beats people-with-laptops stock art; the brain pauses to decode it and that pause builds memory. Also drop in-jargon references (a pen tool in a logo) that the user would not understand.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([Reviewing LEAST UPVOTED Subscriber LOGO Designs! Ep 23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTq-ukxd2CI)); SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Design FULL Course: Advanced Graphic Design Course!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nKoNZ1u21ss)); willpatersondesign ([Logo Design Subscriber Critique Ep 18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fctkGnnIU_0))

## What makes a product feel premium
### Spend effort on evidence of quality, not another hero
TimGabe cites packaging studies from 2010 and 2021: the look gets you picked, evidence of a good product gets you called premium. AI-assisted products all look decent and alike, which caps price (the "commodity ceiling"). Evidence lives in handled states, consistency across the whole product, undo, and graceful failure.
- Look for: pretty hero and dashboard, but bare empty states, no undo, no recovery, generic errors.
- Fix: put polish time into states, recovery and consistency first. Mews cites: kiosk upsells rose over 50 percent in a year after redesigning every microcopy line and microinteraction, and NPS rose from 3 to about 31 (speaker's figures). Treat checkout, upsell and confirmation screens as revenue surfaces.
Sources: TimGabe ([The Psychology of Premium Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8)); Hatch Conference ([The Path to zero Screens - Juan Hidalgo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fb7XJo0EyaE))

### Review for the states the generator skipped
TimGabe cites mid-2026 research giving the same design tasks to five AI building tools: they explained design choices confidently, but about a quarter of that reasoning never reached the interface. The skipped parts were system status, undo and error recovery ("design theater"). Add a dedicated pass for loading, error, empty, undo and success.
- Look for: "did the tap work?" and "can I go back?" moments. Easy-to-process products are judged more kindly, and people under-report how much a clumsy product slows them (speaker cites decades of research).
- Fix: immediate feedback on every action and a visible undo.
Sources: TimGabe ([The Psychology of Premium Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8))

### Give the same situation the same component and copy
When two flows end in the same situation (out of sessions, limit reached), they get one component and one string. Inconsistent twins are the signature of AI-generated screen sprawl.
- Look for: grep the codebase for duplicate empty, limit and error components and merge them.
Sources: TimGabe ([The Psychology of Premium Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8))

### Add a sensory layer around a commodity feature
Day One charges for journaling while Apple Notes is free. It enriches around the text: auto-captured weather, location on a map, embedded photos, a timeline of years, polished search. "Craft is the work users will not see but will feel": animation timing, font weight, metadata appearing at the right place and time. Compare a plain notebook to a Moleskine with pocket, band and ribbon.
- Fix: for a CRUD feature that competitors share, list the context you can auto-fill and show (timestamps, source, preview, history) and tune micro-timings.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top 1% Indie Apps Beats Giants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MAvKHToGiVY))

### Put personality on the core surface
A friendly empty state, confetti on first save or a mascot on loading is decoration if the main screen is still a chart or table. Carrot Weather made the character's reaction the forecast headline, with the same data as Apple Weather underneath.
- Fix: on the main screen, make the interpretation the headline and the raw numbers secondary.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top 1% Indie Apps Beats Giants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MAvKHToGiVY))

### Burn down the papercut list and schedule polish loops
Every product has small known-bad things that never reach the top of the backlog (one slow page, harsh default colors). After FigJam launched, the team spent years iterating on snapping, alignment, color ranges (so they were not garish), image borders so images hold on canvas, and toolbar behavior. Craft is taste (seeing good versus great), skill to close the gap, and repeated cycles.
- Fix: keep a papercut list including performance annoyances, clear it in batches. After each build, compare to a best-in-class reference, name the gap, iterate again. Flag visible rough edges (misaligned elements, placeholder copy, default focus rings, unstyled empty states) as defects, not later polish.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Jenny Wen: The design process is dead. Why Designers can no longer trust it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4u94juYwLLM)); Hatch Conference ([How Notion's Design Team Makes Great Products Quickly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8odhmX32w2M)); Hatch Conference ([The Path to zero Screens - Juan Hidalgo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fb7XJo0EyaE))

### Make the first minutes feel expensive
Revolut's onboarding uses rich imagery and smooth transitions instead of a bare signup form, so it feels high-end from the start. Put the best visual and animation work into signup, first run and first success. Premium positioning cues from critiques: two neutrals plus one deep or metallic accent, one clean sans, generous padding, and the same level of care in small pieces (empty states, emails, receipts).
Sources: TimGabe ([The Secret Behind Weirdly Addictive Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du2lkZ_cux8)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber LOGO Designs! Ep 13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GGY2vUow_vk))

### Doing less reads as more expensive
"It is surprising how easy it is to make something look more professional by just not doing much to it." Per pass, remove one decoration, one color and one border; keep each removal if nothing important is lost. Cut stars, arrows, separator lines, scribbles and clip-art that do not carry the message. Replace dividers with space or a faint tone change. Do not use minimal as a trend: whitespace is the result of removing excess, and a busy layout is right only when the brief calls for it.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([Redesigning Your Logos! (Most Common Mistakes) Ep8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-bwYtTjcjqc)); willpatersondesign ([5 Pro Tips For Minimal Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B-F7H1xVcec)); uxpeak ([This UI/UX Redesign Will Teach You More Than 100 Tutorials Combined](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GGg61sdEjeI)); SatoriGraphics ([The Minimal Design MYTH! (What Is Minimal Graphic Design?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I1V5g73p0W8))

## Getting AI-built UI off the average
### Write a specific direction, not more references
TimGabe cites a Contra study: on vague briefs the models with the best taste won, but once given a specific creative direction those models lost to one that simply followed direction well. So the ceiling is the direction. Feeding every reference, design system and image at once produced a "Frankenstein" (mismatched components, haywire spacing and font sizes) that scored below the plain prompt.
- Fix: before generating, write the one idea, voice, palette, density and what to avoid. Give one coherent direction and a small token set. Cut references rather than add. Write follow-ups as specific diffs ("give these sections more space", name the layout, hierarchy, type, spacing, color or contrast issue) instead of "make it better".
Sources: TimGabe ([The Only Formula Behind Truly Great Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA)); amirmoradiUX ([How to Become a Product Designer in the AI Era [Full Course]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ky_F3jdnT4Y))

### Treat the first draft as the first 10 to 20 percent
One designer keeps only 10 to 20 percent of what an AI builder suggests, then sketches layout, headlines and flow by hand; a page's structure matters as much as its content. Another source: AI builders do not know the customer journey (testimonials before or after contact form), misread the business (mountain photos on a landscaping page), and leave links and menus unwired.
- Fix: replace placeholder imagery with domain-correct assets, wire every button and link, and choose section order from the user journey. Panel view: one person plus AI does about 80 percent; the last 20 percent (spacing rhythm, copy, empty states, hover and focus) is where products win.
Sources: DesignSpo ([Are AI Web Design Tools Worth It?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAu45BXMIjE)); Panel ([AI is Redesigning Design Tools - with Lovable, V0 and Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rrt_MDrpraU)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Seismic Shifts Happening In Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oY87jJx4sAI))

### Make the generator reuse your system, then grep
Give the agent the existing components and tokens, then verify the diff reuses them instead of making lookalikes. Messy component names and tokens in the source produce worse output.
- Fix: point at the component library or tokens file, then grep the diff for hand-rolled buttons and inputs and hardcoded colors.
Sources: amirmoradiUX ([How to Become a Product Designer in the AI Era [Full Course]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ky_F3jdnT4Y)); amirmoradiUX ([How I Use AI as a Product Designer (4 Real Workflows)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OEjBGWPvA3I))

### Run a quick template-to-unique pass
After scaffolding, run in order: palette from the product's meaning, replace fonts, change corner style, add a custom background pattern, delete unused sections, use bespoke imagery with cutouts and shadows. Even a quick pass changes how expensive a site feels. When asked to "make it look better", run a short list (break up text, consistent spacing, line-height ratios, one font superfamily, palette from references, subtle texture, squint test) instead of restyling at random.
Sources: TimGabe ([How To Design a $5k Website in 5 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hCKdIwbhS-s)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([9 Web Design Hacks I Wish I Knew Years Ago](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeMtV8BaZt0))

### Add one or two human details
Signs a person touched it: a hand-drawn arrow or underline, a rough or phone-shot real photo instead of perfect stock, paper or ink texture, a sketch-like illustration. Keep it to one or two imperfect elements and subtle asymmetry. Perfume hero example: the bottle overlapped the headline, brand text faded in the top right, a thin line ran from the bottle to price and details. Match polish to context: developer tools lean clean and tight, consumer or creative brands carry more texture, and functional UI (forms, tables) stays clean either way. Build clean first, then add roughness as a separate layer that can be switched off.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Steal These Web Design Trends 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=waHuVF3XuMA)); SatoriGraphics ([How A Graphic Designer MUST Think in 2026 To Survive!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFkGbyH24AY)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber DESIGN Work Ep 35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjx2c9wyxVE))

### Reset the interface for the niche, do not reskin the category
A planner for ADHD users replaced text rows with checkboxes by colored, illustrated time blocks sized by duration on a vertical timeline; mass-market leaders cannot follow because their audience is too broad. Cal AI made the camera the home screen and correction the exception, instead of add food, search, scroll, select, confirm. MacroFactor assumed users will miss targets and used a smoothed trend instead of red overages and broken streaks.
- Fix: before copying a dashboard or list pattern, ask what the category assumes about its user and whether it is true; ask if a different primitive (timeline, blocks, canvas, camera) fits. When adding AI, redesign the entry point rather than adding an "AI" button to the old flow. Audit for guilt UI (red overages, scolding notifications). For AI features, avoid the default chatbox, sparkle button and purple; return the right component (cards, table, timeline) instead of a wall of markdown.
Sources: TimGabe ([The Weird Design Playbook of 6 App Outliers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q)); hatchconference ([Matteo Loglio: Intelligence as a Material](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qIYeZ7b4e7k))

### Model the user's body and context
A human-designed alarm app had no snooze and only stopped after shaking the phone for 15 seconds with a filling bar, because the real goal is waking up. The AI version did not think of the bed or the half-asleep arm. Same app: one governing idea ("waking up gently") visible on every screen through a sunrise gradient, casual human copy and a clear numeric hierarchy.
- Fix: for each core flow ask whether completing the UI action achieves the real-world goal. Write a one-line concept and check each screen for a repeated trace of it (visual, copy voice, motion).
Sources: TimGabe ([The Only Formula Behind Truly Great Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA))

### Automate the skipped etiquette and remove performance pressure
Loom found users skipped optional "packaging" (title, description) though viewers needed it; auto titles, summaries and chapters fixed it. Features that handed work back (trim, stitch) went unused. Loom also removes filler words and silences so creators can ramble in one take.
- Fix: find optional steps users routinely skip that matter downstream and auto-fill them with an editable default. In creation flows add forgiving defaults (auto-clean, undo, drafts).
Sources: hatchconference ([How Designers Can Build Real Connection in an AI Driven Workplace from Loom's VP of Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aH3GHVnL7YU))

## Where to be distinctive and where to stay familiar
### Keep conventions for finding things, spend originality in a few places
Prototypical layouts are easier to use; if everything is typical it looks generic. Share dialogs look the same across collaborative products and that helps. A design that is 100 percent derived feels like a worse version of something loved.
- Fix: keep standard patterns for nav, forms, checkout, settings, sharing, billing, tables. Put the distinctive work in places users remember: hero, onboarding, empty states, copy voice, illustration, brand motion. Do not spend time reinventing the date picker, accordion or tabs. Pull references from editorial sites, magazines and outside software (watches, cars, film, architecture) instead of SaaS template galleries.
Sources: DesignSpo ([How to Make Your Website Look PROFESSIONAL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtjOJkmnzs8)); Panel ([AI is Redesigning Design Tools - with Lovable, V0 and Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rrt_MDrpraU)); Brad Frost ([Is Atomic Design Dead? - Hatch Conference Berlin 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PK_PICNTgAg)); Hatch Conference ([Designing the Design Process: Lessons from Grammarly's VP of Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6UcZ8pyYakg))

### Break a pattern only when repetition surrounds it
Breaking a pattern works only inside a field of repetition. On an angular site the single circular hover area drew the eye; with no surrounding repetition a break just looks like an error. Lloyds Bank (black and green) stands out among UK banks using black, red or blue. One odd element steals attention, so decide which one it is.
- Fix: make all cards and buttons one shape logic, then make the single hero CTA the odd one. Change one thing at a time. Also useful: psychological contrast (an unexpected typeface for the category, a playful voice in a serious domain) once you know the genre norms. Allow a deliberate rule break only with a one-line reason tied to brand or message.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Unlock The Power Of This One Design Principle!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WtrsoVRJAVE)); SatoriGraphics ([Master 5 Design Principles With This Course! (MUST WATCH)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FpwZTl3dCZE)); SatoriGraphics ([How to BREAK Graphic Design Rules!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pEENvfgCEF0)); SatoriGraphics ([Full Course: 3 PROVEN Principles Of Layout Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GT-K0CrrgfU))

### Pick one opinionated interaction and keep it
Cash App uses a custom payment keypad instead of the native keyboard (maybe more taps) because it fits how people think about money, plus loud greens, bold shapes and type with presence. Speaker says this distinction is why people remember it. Pick one signature interaction or visual trait and keep it consistent; avoid the beige-and-gray default and the same card grid every AI tool produces.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Cam Worboys: How AI is Redefining the Role of UX and Product Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KyOMQJUxQ28))

### Design for one audience in their words
Great concepts come from knowing one audience well enough to speak its language. Name the single target user per page and use their vocabulary in headlines and examples, not generic SaaS copy. Write a "must show" and "must avoid" list per screen before designing (dashboard: key numbers at a glance; avoid decorative animation, irrelevant graphics, clutter).
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Change How You Think As A Designer, With Just ONE Video!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4PHzHgDjiu4)); SatoriGraphics ([I Wish I Knew These Graphic Design Rules SOONER!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=05EKAJPRrGw))

### Write a moodboard and trade-off principles before generating
Collect 3 textures, 3 references and 3 adjectives as constraints; patterns appear and become the brand's DNA. Principles are only useful if they settle a trade-off between two good things ("we prioritize X over Y, because ..."); delete any that nobody would argue against.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([How I Designed A Clothing Brand From Scratch! (Full Process)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=awe5yCp7mjA)); hatchconference ([Slava Shestopalov: Moving away from hands-on Work as a Design Leader](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNIohRhMlDI))

### Treat trends as seasoning
Use at most one trend element per page and justify it by the product. Speaker's 2026 list: maximalism, annotation lines and mono labels (good for technical products), accessible WebGL, a pinch of internet nostalgia, opt-in micro-sounds. The soft purple, blue or teal glow gradient is the SaaS uniform because it is easy; if it is your default, replace it with a brand-specific palette or shape. 3D gradients and pixel art date quickly; build identity on type, spacing and one accent. Before adopting a trend (overlapping shapes, line art), test it small and in one color.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Steal These Web Design Trends 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=waHuVF3XuMA)); willpatersondesign ([6 Huge Beginner Logo Design Mistakes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79scIUNfW-s)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing INSANE Subscriber LOGO Designs Ep 14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cG9JDZQK5kM))

## Consistency across many screens
### Compare screen 1 with screen N, and audit variants in a table
Compare the first and last screen for drift in spacing, radius, icon weight, button style and tone of voice. For one repeated element (avatar menu, primary button, empty state, modal header), screenshot every instance, tabulate where each is used, which is legacy versus new, and collapse to one proposal. Flag any that exist only from drift.
- Look for: raw hex, rgb() and magic pixel values in components (a sign of missing components); form validation built as ad hoc banners instead of one component.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([I Wish I Knew These Graphic Design Rules SOONER!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=05EKAJPRrGw)); hatchconference ([Slava Shestopalov: Moving away from hands-on Work as a Design Leader](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNIohRhMlDI)); Hatch Conference ([Building design systems that work for your whole Organisation  – Sarrah Vesselov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ljl0aUdcpg))

### Stress-test the system on three formats
Apply the brand (logo, color, font, icons) to a social card, a deck slide and a product mockup. If it holds in all three it is a system; if it falls apart it was only a style. Render it in the app UI, an OG card and an email, and test the logo at 16px favicon size and on light and dark backgrounds. Consistency also reaches marketing: Duolingo's marketing video reuses the app's UI sounds and portrait framing; Linear's site header mirrors the app's composition.
- Fix: reuse product UI patterns, labels and layout in landing screenshots and hero art. Never promise a look or feature the app does not have.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([Why Your Designs Still Look Amateur (And How Pros Think Differently)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UtEnQCWd0m4)); SatoriGraphics ([How To Use Advanced Graphic Design Principles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2RgNy94-No)); Hatch Conference ([Adrien Griveau: How Linear designs for Planners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJCu7BPvSw))

### Pick one shape logic and apply it everywhere
Instead of adding variety, choose one corner style, angle or curve and apply it to buttons, cards, images, inputs, icons, chips and highlights. Coherence reads as intentional; variety reads as a fight between ideas. Match the radius, border opacity and shadow of images to cards. Rounded ends and corners read friendly, sharp flat ends read professional; pick one cap style (`stroke-linecap`) and keep it. "Playfully serious" for an AI tool: moderate radii (not pill-everything, not sharp) and a neutral grotesque for UI text with one distinctive display touch.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video That Will Make You A Better Designer In ONE DAY!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exYfWADm-Ns)); DesignSpo ([I Redesigned YOUR Websites - Episode. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oO8MzIVyiv0)); willpatersondesign ([Redesigning Your Logos! (Common Mistakes) Ep4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WKU7oRKLlZE)); Will Paterson Design ([From Sketch to Logo: My Full Process (Real Client Work)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zipw9sx9Hy4))

### Match icon stroke to type weight, and one image style per page
Line art thinner than the wordmark looks disconnected and vanishes small. Match icon stroke to the adjacent font weight and keep every illustration at the same line thickness. Keep icon sets to one style (filled only for the selected item) and social icons in one set, size and color. Keep one image style per page (photo, flat or 3D); photos on a flat-illustration page clash. For a catalog, fix aspect ratio, `object-fit` and background so uploads read as one system.
- Look for: mixed social icon styles, filled and outline icons in one bar, photos with a different background and lighting per card.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([Pro Logo Designer VS Fiverr Designers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TKdsKBd7Eo8)); uxpeak ([Top UI/UX Design Tips - How to Design a Great Bottom Mobile Navigation Bar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLJ40GV2XEc)); uxpeak ([This UI/UX Redesign Will Teach You More Than 100 Tutorials Combined](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GGg61sdEjeI)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS To Beautiful Landing Page Design (Pt. 3: Desktop Workflow)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vBuyR8uF380))

### Reuse the proven page template for parent and child objects
Linear reused its project layout (summary, work list, side reports) for "initiatives" above projects, so users felt at home. When adding a parent or child object, clone the existing page template (header, tabs, list, side panel) instead of inventing a layout. Consistency also speeds adoption: TimGabe cites iOS 17 reaching 77 percent adoption in 6 months versus Android 14 at 13 percent after a year, attributed to consistent, learnable design.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Adrien Griveau: How Linear designs for Planners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJCu7BPvSw)); TimGabe ([What Founders Can Learn From Apple's Design Domination](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8))

### Remove style debates with opinionated defaults
Prettier ends style debates by enforcing one preset. Wise predefined every title distance, font and component state in its screen-assembly tool so non-designers build precise screens fast. A design system exists so nobody argues which component or token to use.
- Fix: ship one spacing scale, one type scale, one set of states. Do not expose per-screen overrides. Lint for off-scale values. Do not design by feel: bigger reads as more important, closer reads as related, so every size and gap needs a scale behind it (a system is a base, not a prison).
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Henrique Gusso - Wise: Designing with the Precision of Code](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BHlWX9HeTtM)); Hatch Conference ([The Path to zero Screens - Juan Hidalgo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fb7XJo0EyaE)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Seismic Shifts Happening In Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oY87jJx4sAI))

### Tokens: adaptive size names and relationship classes
Name spacing xs, s, m, l, xl and keep each name across breakpoints while the value changes (`--space-xl` is 32px on mobile, larger on desktop, redefined inside `@media`, still multiples of 4). Add layers: utility (padding-large), component (section-default = max-width + padding + gap), and relationship (label with field, two buttons side by side, heading with its content) such as `.stack`, `.cluster`, `.field`. Changing section padding then needs one edit and related things never drift apart. Custom scales are fine for a few elements (button sizes, fluid hero headings) once the base system exists.
Sources: DesignSpo ([How To Easily Design Pixel Perfect Websites (7-Step Process)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bDC6JM0sTI)); DesignSpo ([How To Build Websites 10x Faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdabjdPJQ94))

### Keep code as the source of truth and grow the system from real use
Define tokens and components in code and sync design tools from code, not the reverse. Name components, props and variants the same in design and code. Extract a component on the second or third real use, not for imagined edge cases; delete unused variants. Document deviations ("snowflakes") in a short list; when the same one-off appears in three places, promote it. Untracked detaching is the failure case. Standardize one component (banner, input, empty state) with each new feature rather than a full rewrite.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([How Notion's Design Team Makes Great Products Quickly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8odhmX32w2M)); Hatch Conference ([(In)consistencies in Design Systems - Julia Kestner (Figma)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5APIo30od6E)); Hatch Conference ([Death to Handovers! - Hatch Conference 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NfC0mRzFZMs)); Hatch Conference ([Building design systems that work for your whole Organisation  – Sarrah Vesselov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ljl0aUdcpg))

### Judge patterns, not just components
Exclusion and friction usually come from how components are assembled (language, defaults, field order, placeholders, error copy), not the button itself. In component docs add when to use, defaults, copy rules and what never to do. Healthy systems let product inform system and system inform product; failure modes are the perfect button nobody uses, "pattern police", and a product that swallows the system. Terminology is a token too: grep the UI for synonyms (Project, Workspace, Space; Delete, Remove; Sign in, Log in), pick one, keep a short glossary.
Sources: hatchconference ([Luke Murphy: Systems of Exclusion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLtVIEcHdSo)); Brad Frost ([Is Atomic Design Dead? - Hatch Conference Berlin 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PK_PICNTgAg)); Jason Giles ([How UserTesting does user testing on UserTesting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-Fqc1t_gE4))

### Consistency is a means, and defaults can be wrong
A rebrand gave four sibling apps near-identical icons, and users could not tell the taxi app from the car-sharing app in a tab bar. Check that related products' icons, favicons and tab titles are distinguishable at 16 to 32px; set usability guard rails the brand cannot cross. Old Material text fields looked disabled and users did not know they could type; if a library default has a known flaw, fix it for the product. Changing one brand token (the orange) means checking every screen for new clashes; prefer a refresh over a rebrand unless the product or audience changed.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([(In)consistencies in Design Systems - Julia Kestner (Figma)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5APIo30od6E)); willpatersondesign ([Why Are Big Brands Secretly Updating Their Logos?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-5osZrFVmIc))

### Keep light and elevation logic global
Light comes from one direction across the product. Use one shadow offset direction and one elevation scale for cards, modals and dropdowns, soft and low-opacity.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([How To Design A Brand Concept 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qMjNyvJNtlU))

## The detail pass users feel but cannot name
### Trust the eye over the box
Aligned and equal-sized boxes can still look off. A centered play triangle sits left of center because its mass is on one side; nudge it 1 to 2px toward the heavy side. Round letters and icons look smaller than straight ones at the same size. A 40 by 40 heart and burst fill space differently. Gaps like 4, 8, 16, 18, 22, 24 can look right if they look aligned: check by eye after the grid check. Use `text-box-trim` where supported.
- Look for: icon plus label in buttons, play and chevron icons in circles, round icon beside square one, vertical centering of button text, logo next to wordmark.

### Design small icons for small sizes
On a large monitor you underestimate how small an icon will be. Make internal gaps and counters larger than they seem to need, use fewer strokes at 16 to 24px than at 64px, and keep a simplified small variant if needed. Build icons on a grid from true circle and rect primitives with few anchor points, preview at 100 percent, and check for blur at 16px. Where a round end meets a straight stem, ease the curve so the join does not pinch.

Pure-black shadows look harsh. Use a tinted dark at low alpha with large blur and small offset (rgba 0.08 to 0.15) and avoid 0.5. If a hero screenshot melts into its background, add a soft shadow or a 1px border. For floating cards and screenshots one soft wide shadow plus a tight heavy one that acts like a border: `0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / .25), 0 12px 32px rgb(0 0 0 / .08)`. Tint shadows toward the background hue and keep light direction consistent across images.

### Add depth by layering, one per section
Let the focal image overlap its container edge by 16 to 40px instead of sitting politely inside. In a cut-out hero, put part of the headline behind the subject with z-index. Cards can overlap a background shape; the first row of the next section can peek into the hero (negative top margin) as a scroll cue. A tiny element far in a corner with a quiet counterweight opposite makes the eye hunt. Offsetting every second card down about 80px breaks the monotony of a card row.
- Look for: layouts where nothing ever crosses a boundary; empty quadrants left by accident (draw a box and check weight in each quadrant).
- Fix: use one layering move per section and keep text readable. Avoid the stagger offset on data tables.

### Squircles and optical radius
Apple uses continuous-curvature corners instead of plain circular radii; subtle, but softer and more expensive. Use `corner-shape: squircle` where supported on cards and app icons, or an SVG or clip-path approach, and keep nested radii consistent. Perfect circles and ellipses are optically wrong, so nudge curves slightly.

### Typography moves that carry feel
Open Sans is the second most popular face on Google Fonts, so using it makes you look like everyone else (speaker's claim); decent paid faces start around 30 dollars. A serif display face with a muted palette made the same tea product feel premium (speaker guessed 4 dollars). Wide letterspacing on uppercase display words reads luxurious, tightening removes that. Keep headline block size roughly equal to the focal image, and secondary text at exact ratios (50 or 75 percent).

### Soft decoration must stay behind the content
If a decorative element is the first thing noticed in the squint test, reduce its opacity or remove it. The speaker's split: structure, type and assets get about 80 percent of the way, and the last 19 percent of details is where pages turn into clutter. Cap decoration at a few repeated treatments and ask whether the page is worse without a flourish. ## Motion and interaction polish
### Design the peak moment: anticipation, reveal, celebration
Most apps show only the reveal, flat ("Congratulations" modal). Give result moments three stages: a short build-up, a reveal with visual weight (sound or haptics where fitting), then a celebration or share moment that lets the result breathe. Sequential reveals beat all-at-once. One case study built a weekly recap that arrives wrapped, a subtle bounce hints at the gesture, the user peels it open, then payoff on two channels (confetti plus subtle haptic), framing ("first entry in your record"), and the next-step ask placed inside the peak. Tone constraint: nothing clinical, nothing cute.
- Fix: pick the one success moment (first report, first export, upgrade, deploy done) and give it a designed entrance, a payoff and the next CTA inside it. Routine saves get a quiet toast. Tinder's match screen with confetti became shareable screenshots. TimGabe cites a Robinhood confetti animation that led to a $7.5M fine: celebratory UI changes behavior, use it responsibly.
Sources: TimGabe ([The 3-Stage Trick Behind Every Addictive App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw)); TimGabe ([How Top 1% Apps Earn Your Second Visit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YA3QpYIKqNI)); TimGabe ([How Tinder's Design Hacked Society](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA))

### Pin emotion to 2 or 3 screens, not every control
Pin emotional moments on specific screens early, then choose those tied to business goals (first success, key action confirmed, milestone). Examples: voice-linked audio waves in a transcription app, an animated logo in the core experience, chart scrubbing with a soft glow in Revolut. Use lightweight, interactive motion (Lottie, Rive, CSS). Speaker cites research that designs evoking positive feeling through motion, feedback and human touches are remembered and recommended more. FigJam's stamps and cursor chat were kept because testers smiled unprompted even while usability problems remained.
Sources: TimGabe ([Our World Class App Design Formula](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wmTkiF23GRQ)); TimGabe ([The Secret Behind Weirdly Addictive Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du2lkZ_cux8)); Hatch Conference ([Jenny Wen: The design process is dead. Why Designers can no longer trust it.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4u94juYwLLM))

### Use springs with named knobs
Spring motion acts like a real object; plain bezier curves feel generic and can stop oddly. Knobs: stiffness (quick or bouncy), damping (more damping, less bounce), mass (more mass, longer and heavier). Presets from the source: aggressive is high stiffness, low damping, low mass; smooth is lower stiffness, more damping, a bit more mass; playful is high stiffness, medium damping, high mass.
- Fix: stiff and quick for toggles, smooth and heavier for sheets and drag, bouncy only for celebration. Rubber-band overscroll, momentum, friction and haptics make a UI feel like an object so users do not need to learn it.
Sources: TimGabe ([The Secret Trick For Amazing Animations in Figma](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdR3p6PvYQQ)); TimGabe ([What Founders Can Learn From Apple's Design Domination](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8)); TimGabe ([Figma Smart Animate for Beginners (2022 Tutorial)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=thplNeqE9Tg))

### Carry the user spatially between screens
Apple rarely swaps screens abruptly; animated transitions let users build a mental map. Animate list-to-detail and modal open from the clicked element's position (shared element transition or the View Transitions API), animate the active-tab indicator's position, and use slide or fade between routes so it does not feel like teleporting. Animate the same element between states (sidebar slide-out, expand) rather than swapping instantly.
Sources: TimGabe ([What Founders Can Learn From Apple's Design Domination](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8)); uxpeak ([Top UI/UX Design Tips - How to Design a Great Bottom Mobile Navigation Bar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLJ40GV2XEc)); TimGabe ([Figma Smart Animate for Beginners (2022 Tutorial)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=thplNeqE9Tg))

### Finish motion with easing, no hard stops
A logo animation that snapped to a stop instead of easing out was flagged, as was linear movement ("flat"). Easing in and out is what makes motion feel finished. Reviewers also asked for less wobble and jitter.
- Look for: a hard stop in the last 100ms of any transition; `linear` on UI transitions.
- Fix: for example `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` on entry and exit, and looseness (slight overshoot) for icon or logo animation instead of rigid moves.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([Reviewing Your Designs On Reddit! Ep2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5k9Vj8sKMG8)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing LEAST UPVOTED Subscriber LOGO Designs! ### Storyboard a sequence before coding it
Lay out each frame of an animation as a numbered list of states with the property that changes in each. Only elements that change between frames need to exist in both; clip content inside a container so elements enter from inside its edge. Simple intro: reveal, counter, text fade-up, nav slides down, then product image. Each animated layer must be a separate, non-nested element, because animating a parent animates all children. Over-size a background by about 150px past the frame when scaling it so the zoom never reveals edges.

### Keep headline text visible at first paint
Start the timeline with the text fully visible so people read it at first glance, then animate. Match animation style to the font and message. Pace long pages like music: a fast high-impact hero, slower dense detail, then a pause with lots of space (punch, slow, pull, release). After a dense section add an airy one before the next push.

### Let motion steer attention, and use gesture cues over tutorials
Animate only the element to be read first and keep side items calmer. A scroll-triggered reveal at the top of a single-column page signals that something will happen below. Teach gestures with a visual cue plus gradual change (the iPhone's bottom line trained swipe-home over about ten years with no tutorial): use drag handles and peeking cards instead of tutorial modals, and phase in big redesigns. Ask what a second sense (haptic, sound) could tell the user that color cannot; keep sound subtle, short and opt-in.

### Small interaction recipes
- Button loading in place: label slides up and out, spinner or dots rise in, button keeps fixed width to avoid layout shift, then restores on result. No separate spinner.
- Rolling-label hover: two stacked spans in an `overflow: hidden` button, old label slides out, new slides in, about 200ms ease-out.
- Line reveal: wrap each headline line in `overflow: hidden` and animate inner `translateY(100%)` to 0.
- Tap feedback: color change, scale or ripple, plus a sliding underline between tabs.
- Hover on a menu: other items fade to 50 percent opacity.
- Tactile data: chart scrub with tooltip and highlight, card tilt or flip on key product visuals (Revolut).

### Sticky feature walkthroughs and their CSS traps
A sticky visual (top offset about 160px) stays in view while text sections scroll, and its variant switches as each section crosses the viewport center. Sticky works only when the parent's height sets the scroll area (for example a 300vh container with a 100vh sticky child) and every ancestor has `overflow: visible`.
- Look for: `position: sticky` doing nothing means an ancestor has `overflow: hidden` or `auto`, or the parent has no extra height. Keep content above the sticky layer with z-index.

## Perceived performance and weight
### Narrate slow work with named steps
For a slow job, name each step as it runs (transcribing, anonymizing, summarizing) with a progress bar, and mirror it outside the app (Dynamic Island, lock screen). Naming steps calms users when the data is sensitive and helps catch forgotten sessions. Send a "ready" notification when done.
- Fix: long-running tasks show named stages, never a bare spinner.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top 1% Apps Earn Your Second Visit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YA3QpYIKqNI))

### Fix images first, they are the main cause of slow pages
Do not upload 6000px PNGs. Resize to display size (a 4K screen needs about half of that), compress lossy (speaker cites 50 to 90 percent savings), and convert to WebP or AVIF; combined, up to 98 percent smaller with a near-identical look. One shown multi-MB PNG became about 100KB WebP. Flag any image above about 300KB. Cap heroes at about 2000px wide and use `srcset` and `sizes`.
Sources: DesignSpo ([Give me 8 minutes and I'll make your website 278% faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mXv4NPp5IPM)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips & Tricks to Level Up Instantly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U))

### Load only the first screen eagerly
Lazy-load everything below the hero: speaker cites 200 to 300 percent faster appearance with the same bytes. Use `loading="lazy"` on below-fold images and iframes and `preload="none"` on video. Never lazy-load the LCP hero image. Reserve space for images, embeds and async content (width and height or `aspect-ratio`) so nothing jumps while someone reads or clicks; check Lighthouse CLS.
Sources: DesignSpo ([Give me 8 minutes and I'll make your website 278% faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mXv4NPp5IPM)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Be A Web Designer in 2026 (Free Resources & My Best Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vYvzOyTA2z4))

### Prune scripts, cache, and set a weight budget
Minify HTML, CSS and JS (about 30 percent faster, speaker cites). Remove unused plugins, themes, scripts, trackers and background processes; the speaker has not found a small-business site without bloat. Use a CDN (speaker cites up to 2x) and long-lived caching on hashed assets (browser cache about 10x faster than the network, speaker cites). Use SVG for icons, one or two font files at most, and Lighthouse to find what slows a page. Ambient effects (grain, shader backgrounds) must stay within the budget.
Sources: DesignSpo ([Give me 8 minutes and I'll make your website 278% faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mXv4NPp5IPM)); hatchconference ([Creating product experiences for a sustainable future – Sebastian Gier](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=noDIQCuHv2g)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips & Tricks to Level Up Instantly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U))

## Where designers disagree
These sources recommend things frontend-design bans or constrains. Default to frontend-design; use these only when the brief clearly wants a brand-led Persuade surface.

### Gradients, glow, grain and glass for depth
- Sources recommend: noise or grain overlay at 0.03 to 0.2 opacity (`soft-light` or `overlay`, not normal blend), mesh or aurora blobs with `blur(80px+)` (150px for hero ovals, opacity about 0.2 to 0.3), dot grid masked by a radial ellipse, glass cards (`blur(12px)`, translucent fill and 1px translucent border, glass only over colorful or image backgrounds), brand-color button glow, blurred hero image reused as a section backdrop, "last 10 percent" ambient texture such as film grain or slow gradient. They argue it makes flat UI feel rich and tactile.
- frontend-design says: no gradients, glows or mesh backgrounds, glassmorphism only as one subtle navbar blur.
- Resolution: a faint dot grid or paper texture on a Persuade page is a judgment call; keep Operate surfaces flat. Others in the notes also warn that gradient, glass and glow date quickly and should not be the identity.

### Button and card depth recipes
- Sources recommend: gradient fill, 1px white or black border, `inset 0 1px 0 rgb(255 255 255 / .2 to .4)` plus a soft drop shadow, card shadow with top-left inner highlight and a 15 percent border, a soft shadow on an icon to hint it is clickable.
- frontend-design says: primary button is solid with no shadow; cards have no shadow (hover only a faint one).
- Resolution: the transferable parts are consistency (same treatment on cards, images and inputs) and subtlety.

### Hover timing and 3D tilt
- Sources recommend: about 300ms ease-out for hover and state changes, 200ms ease-in-out with a 5px offset on button hover, a toned-down 3D tilt on hover.
- frontend-design says: 100 to 150ms for hover and press; no 3D tilt cards.

### Longer entrances, parallax and ambient loops
- Sources recommend: hero intros with zoom over about 2s and staggers of 0.3 to 0.5s, spring scroll reveals with 24px offset, parallax on one element (150 percent against 100 percent speed), scroll-linked scale on a headline, slow mirrored decorative loops (about minus 10 to 10 degrees, 2s) and small rotation in entry states.
- frontend-design says: one standard entrance at 600ms with 12px travel, stagger 80 to 100ms; no parallax, no loops of micro-animations in the hero; no scroll-position-driven reveals.
- Resolution: both agree reveals must be limited to one preset reused everywhere, with `prefers-reduced-motion` honored.

### Cursor-follow, italics, off-grid type and pill fields
- Sources recommend: an element that follows the cursor, italic or oblique type for motion, off-grid headings that move on scroll (the same source warns of legibility risk), a pill email field with an attached rounded button, a looping effect on a CTA.
- frontend-design says: no custom cursors, no italics, no `rounded-full` on primary buttons.

### Consistency strictness
- Sources say: perfect consistency is not the goal. Documented deviations are healthy, mathematical consistency can look uneven, and sibling products must stay distinguishable. A deliberately messy brand is fine for consumer or artistic audiences.
- frontend-design says: match the project's existing system and keep every value exact and in role.
- Resolution: deviate only with a documented reason, and judge by eye after the token check.
