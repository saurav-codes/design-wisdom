# Conversion and Copy

Open this when building or reviewing a landing page, pricing or paywall screen, or the words inside a product (buttons, labels, prompts). It covers what to test, what to put in the hero, how to order sections, how to show proof, how to price, and how to write short.

## Tests to run on the real page
### Run the 5-second stranger test
Show the hero for 5 seconds (or cover everything below it), then ask: what is this, who is it for, what do I do next, why should I care. Speakers claim most visitors only look at the top of the page and that trust is judged in about 50 ms (Google research, as cited). The first screen has four jobs: grab attention, say what the site is, make people want to act, show the exact next step.
- Look for: clever taglines that omit the thing itself (product category, user, place). Qualifiers buried below the fold.
- Fix: H1 plus subhead name the category and the target user in plain words. Check at 1440x900 and on a phone.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([11 Years of Brutally Honest Web Design Advice in 7 Minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxZHUe8mvhU)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Best Way to Market Your Web Design Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLyf8XvwNgw)); hatchconference ([Designing Products for Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rq9djF2dqI))

### Read only the H2s
Section headings alone should tell the whole story. Give every section one big idea no longer than a tweet, because visitors mostly read the large text while scrolling. Draft the story in a doc first; about half gets cut.
- Look for: H2s that are feature names or labels ("Features", "Why us") instead of claims.
- Fix: each H2 is a one-line takeaway. Paragraphs 2-3 lines, bullets with spacing, answer first (no recipe-blog essays before the recipe).
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Build a UX Design Portfolio that Get's You Hired w/ Sarah Doody](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDPZOqMoS9g))

### Swap-the-logo and shelf tests
Swap-the-logo: if you could replace your logo with another company's and ship the same hero (title, subtitle, image, button aligned like soldiers), it is generic. Shelf test: screenshot your landing and pricing pages in a grid next to five competitors. If yours is indistinguishable, add one distinctive element. Very minimal can look great to designers and still vanish among competitors.
- Fix: follow category conventions (pricing table, nav) but differ visibly in one thing: color, imagery or tone.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([How A Graphic Designer MUST Think in 2026 To Survive!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFkGbyH24AY)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing CLEAN Subscriber DESIGN work Ep 32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EzeqtbjPF3E)); SatoriGraphics ([Do ONE Thing For BETTER Graphic Designs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HlNwpIr1RLg))

### Trace the eye path 1-2-3
Screenshot the hero and mark where attention lands first, where it goes next, where it ends. The CTA belongs at the end of that path. Something must connect the steps (a shape, a color, a gaze). One speaker bets about 90% of viewers hit the left illustration first. Photos whose subject looks at the headline or CTA steer the viewer there.
- Look for: a tiny corner CTA, or a hero image where the person looks away from the text.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([5 Pro Techniques You're FORGETTING!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bsbFFRU5eck)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video On Visual Hierarchy ANY Graphic Designer Needs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4QNEDhrsRLo)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips & Tricks to Level Up Instantly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U))

### Map every moment of hesitation
Walk signup, permission prompts, checkout and billing. List each point where the user must give something (data, permission, belief, money, attention). Each is a trust leap. Write the question they silently ask, then answer it in one line right at that point.
- Look for: asks with no "why we need this" line, or a bigger request than needed.
- Fix: minimum request plus a short reason. Also list the top objections (price, setup, security, switching cost) and give each a one-line answer near the CTA. Cut the rest of the copy.
Sources: hatchconference ([Designing Products for Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rq9djF2dqI)); TimGabe ([Give Me 10 Minutes & I'll Boost Your Website Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs))

### Name the funnel stage and the cause before redesigning
"Redesign the pricing page" is a solution, not a problem. Many visitors and few upgrades could mean unclear plans, a price that feels high, an unclear CTA, or that the pricing page is not the problem. State the metric, the suspected cause and the funnel stage, then design for that one. Change one variable per round (headline, CTA, proof) and find where the funnel breaks before adding traffic or sections.
- Look for: a redesign note that cannot name the leading metric it moves. That is decoration.
Sources: amirmoradiUX ([The Book That Changed My Design Career](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPM_uhuIVe8)); hatchconference ([Kevin Hawkins, Speaking to Stakeholders](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ppAIzybHoCQ)); Jaime Levy ([Getting Lost in Discovery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WwrgZ0hYa9A)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Be A Web Designer in 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vYvzOyTA2z4))

### Never approve a prettier page that is less clear
Two speakers describe the same mistake: a cleaner, more modern client redesign that cut sales because the value proposition, next step and objections got weaker. Before shipping, compare old and new on funnel steps and key-event rates, not looks.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([Give Me 7 Minutes & Your Web Design Skills Will Take Off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NTKwpAVcHg)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Only 5 Web Design Skills That Actually Matter (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vbFn0C-pvis))

### Write the headline and hard questions first
Amazon-style PR/FAQ: write the announcement headline as if it succeeded, then the 3 hardest customer questions with answers. They become the hero copy, the FAQ block and the objection handling. Use real copy in mockups, never lorem ipsum: text and assets are most of a layout, and a design that works with two-word labels can break with four.
Sources: hatchconference ([Kevin Hawkins, Speaking to Stakeholders](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ppAIzybHoCQ)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS to BEAUTIFUL Landing Page Design Pt. 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg)); DesignSpo ([How To Build A Website From Start To Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b2TP7K9ZFWk))

### Check thumbnails at 150-200px
Most people skim small previews before looking closer. Check OG images, template previews and screenshot cards at 150-200px wide. Crop in on the product UI and drop the decorative device frame. The "billboard test": it should impress from several feet away.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Make a Winning Upwork Profile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmDMg88oJXo))

## Hero
### Headline states the outcome, with an audience label
Write the headline as what the visitor wants, in the words they would search for ("How to learn web design fast"). Name the user's situation in a small label above it ("If you're an adult living with ADHD"). The subhead says what, why and how. Avoid cliches ("home away from home") and a skill title ("web designer") when an outcome works ("conversion-focused web designer", "Get paid in 2 days").
- Look for: "Welcome", a product-name-only H1, a headline nobody would type into a search box.
- Fix: eyebrow = who it is for; H1 = result; button verb matches the H1.
Sources: DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Easiest Way to Build Killer Websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O7MaPnRy5Us)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([I Made $80k in Web Design in My Free Time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3lwekQLdI))

### Match the message to where the visitor came from
For ad or channel traffic, the headline says exactly what they clicked (the keyword), location or context sits small above it, and benefits plus guarantee collapse into one paragraph. Keep visuals continuous with the source (ad, channel graphics, a real face for a personal-brand audience). Reassure first, sell second.
- Fix: one CTA that matches the ad ("Book appointment"). Test that hero, pricing tier names and in-app labels use the same words.
Sources: DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Seismic Shifts Happening In Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oY87jJx4sAI)); Jason Giles ([How UserTesting does user testing on UserTesting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-Fqc1t_gE4))

### Sell the bigger problem, and the outcome not the spec
"Having a website" is a tiny problem. Valuable ones: differentiate, earn trust, convert more leads, raise value per customer. Rewrite feature headings as results: "1,000 songs in your pocket" not "1GB storage"; "close tickets 3x faster without switching tabs" not "real-time notifications with Slack and Jira integrations". Keep integration names as supporting proof.
Sources: TimGabe ([Give Me 10 Minutes & I'll Boost Your Website Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Win $10,000 Projects as a Freelance Web Designer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CWIlmDd6I50))

### Write the hero opposite to the category assumption
List what the top 3 competitor sites all say or assume. Circle the assumption their revenue depends on. Make the headline the opposite promise, in a few words (Timeleft: "No bios, no swiping, no planning"; Evidation: use the watch you own).
Sources: TimGabe ([The Secret Behind Apps Big Players Can't Copy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU))

### Keep the hero copy to length limits
Heading 40-60 characters, body at most about 140-170. Lint it. Cut anything that is only context the visitor already has. Pick one asset style (real photo for people-centred products, 3D for tech, plain illustration or animation otherwise) and keep it across the site.
Sources: TimGabe ([Steal This Landing Page Hero Workflow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA)); TimGabe ([4 Easy Tips For Stunning Landing Pages](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cocnZubvfFE))

### Show the real product, not decorative art
Speaker cites own tests on an e-book offer: cover on plain background converted, value points around it reached 22%, real pages of the book reached 48%. A paywall hero with real game characters beat a pretty space illustration because people cannot commit to something they cannot visualize. For software, use a video or interactive demo with a visible play button, or a real UI in a browser or device frame with real data. Generic stock-illustration heroes (camping, CRM) say nothing about the brand.
- Look for: abstract art, stock photos, a grainy unbranded product shot, an illustration style seen "millions of times".
- Fix: product UI, sample output, or preview of the deliverable as the focal point; one hero visual that tells the outcome story.
Sources: uxpeak ([Top 5 UX/UI Design Tips and Tricks, part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8pMUkEbAM7g)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA)); SatoriGraphics ([Create Stunning Designs FAST with These Essential Rules!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gKDK5QFheSQ)); willpatersondesign ([5 Pro Tips For Minimal Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B-F7H1xVcec))

### Let people use it before signup
Put a live mini editor or sandbox on the page and ask for signup only when they save or publish. One speaker says nailing the demo section earns about 80% of the sale. For scanners and generators, show a useful partial result free (score and top issues), then gate the full report. Do not blur what the user just produced. Speaker cites: Cialdini ranks reciprocity as the strongest persuasion driver.
- Fix: before/after demos work too: headline names the thing replaced, demo lets the visitor feel the relief. Tabs above a demo window handle many frameworks without space.
Sources: DesignSpo ([21 Website Layouts You Should Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZGR72wgOT4)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Layouts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0Pk3OVbxRU)); uxpeak ([The UX Psychology Behind Apps People Can't Stop Using](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TlIg3VokY8))

### End the hero on plain reassurances
Three short factual lines under the CTA: speed, price, risk reversal ("Card issued in seconds. No annual fees. Refund anytime."). Checkmarks work for the same job (free trial, no card, cancel anytime). They answer a nervous first-timer before they ask.
Sources: TimGabe ([I Redesigned a Neobank App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GMQRCnOQmo)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA))

### Match tone to the mood the visitor arrives in
A cautious visitor (charity, billing, money) needs softer tone, more space and a trust cue. A shouting headline assumes they are already convinced. Write one line per page: who arrives and how they feel (worried about cost, in a hurry, curious). Adjust tone, density and palette to that. For money, health and security, a dated or rushed site fails the "will they be around in a year" question, so plain claims and real screenshots beat trendy effects.
Sources: SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video You Need To Learn High-Level Design Thinking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzQl1Tl_LPM)); TimGabe ([I Redesigned a Neobank App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GMQRCnOQmo)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video That Shows You How Expert Designers Think!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=brzjHzzxD0E))

## CTAs
### One goal and one primary CTA per page
Name the page's single conversion before building. Speaker cites an Unbounce analysis of 18,000+ landing pages: one CTA converted 3.5%, two 11.9%, three 10.5%; the same speaker still argues for one primary plus one quiet secondary for visitors who need context ("Start free" and "Watch demo"). Cited jam-style study: 24 flavors, 3% bought; 6 flavors, 30% bought. Repeat the same CTA (same label, same destination) down the page instead of adding new ones.
- Look for: five CTAs (contact, get started, book, demo, newsletter) in the nav and hero.
- Fix: nav has links plus one button. Hero has one filled primary and optionally one outline or text secondary. Nothing third.
Sources: TimGabe ([Give Me 10 Minutes & I'll Boost Your Website Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs)); DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E)); ShantanuKumar ([How to Design Amazing Landing pages w/ Readymag.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dro9e5_VT2U)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Only 5 Web Design Skills That Actually Matter (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vbFn0C-pvis))

### Repeat the CTA at three places
Hero, nav (sticky), and again after each major proof or feature block, ending with a large CTA near UI proof at the bottom. Use identical size, color and position of the primary action across pages in a flow.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Only 5 Web Design Skills That Actually Matter (2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vbFn0C-pvis)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS To Beautiful Landing Page Design Pt. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cmSBbnrFGPg)); SatoriGraphics ([How To Master This One Important Design Principle!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB4ZXJVcl6w))

### Make the primary CTA loud, filled and isolated
Ghost buttons are overlooked, so the main CTA is never outline-only. Give it its own clear space (12-16px vertical padding, 16-24px clear around), a color used nowhere else, and contrast of color, size or shape. Where several options are offered, the bigger one gets clicked more. Check by squint that it is the first interactive thing seen. If the brand base is calm trust blue, reserve one warm accent for conversion actions (American Express cited).
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([11 years of web design knowledge in 7 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ityZtG3FMgw)); SatoriGraphics ([6 Golden Rules Of Layout Design You MUST OBEY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EFf9jBs2yfU)); SatoriGraphics ([Making Designs BETTER In Seconds!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y3lcMq0yfjM)); SatoriGraphics ([How To MASTER Brand Design in Just 8 Minutes!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8U8_WbgO9hg))

### Label the CTA with the action, outcome and number
Replace "Learn more", "Submit", "Click here", "Contact", "Continue" with what happens. Examples from the sources: "Get your free quote", "See pricing", "Download the guide", "Show 41 homes" (not "Apply"), "Reserve 445 euros total" (not "Reserve"), "Start my free trial" (not "Subscribe and start 7 days free"), "Start in two taps" (not "quick setup"). Lighter verbs ("Start") and "my" imply beginning and ownership; this is the speaker's reading, not a study. Each label must answer "what happens when I click?".
- Look for: grep buttons for vague labels.
Sources: uxpeak ([Top 3 UX/UI Redesigns That Make You Design Like a Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr37ibqXl1U)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([50 Web Design Tips & Tricks to Level Up Instantly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRFD6tENX2U)); DesignSpo ([I Redesigned YOUR Websites - Episode. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oO8MzIVyiv0)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Easiest Way to Build Killer Websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O7MaPnRy5Us))

### Put the first signup field in the CTA
Instead of a button that opens a form, put the one needed field (email, domain) next to the button in the hero or footer CTA. It removes a step. For pre-launch pages, a single email field with a clear promise.
Sources: DesignSpo ([21 Website Layouts You Should Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZGR72wgOT4)); ShantanuKumar ([How to design a simple website (without coding) - Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o8S9qJbvpkU))

### Give not-ready visitors a lighter exit
A chat widget, "talk to us" link or save-for-later keeps undecided visitors. One speaker signed up because a chat bubble was easier than hunting for an email. Keep it visually lighter than the main CTA. Put it on pricing and signup pages. Show real contact info (phone, email, address as text with icons) instead of "send an email" buttons.
Sources: TimGabe ([Give Me 10 Minutes & I'll Boost Your Website Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs)); DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E))

### Ask for upgrade, rating or share right after a win
Peak-end rule (as cited): people judge by the most intense moment and the ending. List the win events (first project done, export succeeded, report generated) and trigger upgrade, review and share prompts there. Never show a paywall or rating prompt on an error or a confusing step. Tinder-style upsell is tied to the situation (a Boost when you get matches), never in the core flow.
Sources: TimGabe ([Give Me 13 Minutes & I'll Grow Your App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ)); TimGabe ([How Tinder's Design Hacked Society](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA))

## Section order and page structure
### Default SaaS landing order
Nav, hero with demo, social proof strip, one big full-width benefit, two-column benefits, one stylized benefit, bento of features (features matter less than benefits), optional mission band, testimonials (around section 7), CTA container, footer. A first homepage can start as nav, hero, value section, social proof, footer. A longer variant adds pricing cards and a contact route after testimonials.
- Look for: opening with a feature list. Side links that pull away from the path to the button.
Sources: DesignSpo ([The Perfect Website Layout For Startups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z7r5RNo-hN0)); ShantanuKumar ([How to Design Amazing Landing pages w/ Readymag.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dro9e5_VT2U)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Easiest Way to Build Killer Websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O7MaPnRy5Us))

### Order the story: pain, answer, proof, ask
Sequence the page so a problem in the reader's life comes first, the product solves it, and they feel it is worth solving now. Selling layouts have a short path: bold hook, selling points in simple steps, then the button. One niche, one kind of service, one clear next step: "a confused mind doesn't buy".
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Build a UX Design Portfolio that Get's You Hired w/ Sarah Doody](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDPZOqMoS9g)); SatoriGraphics ([The Graphic Design Course To MASTER Layout Design!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yUvGHNzqG7M)); DesignSpo ([$1,000,000 Design Portfolios](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DaeEKKSpgXU))

### Features describe, benefits sell, and the visual says which feature
Card heading = feature, body = outcome ("Auto-reports", then "Stop building Monday slides by hand"). Flag any card that lists only a capability. Use large labeled product visuals, animated or interactive where possible (before/after, case study), so it is clearly "the calendar" or "the timer". Show concrete everyday uses, not abstract lists. A bento grid (varied tile sizes, one idea per tile, consistent gaps) fits the features overview.
- Fix: do not assume visitors know how it works. A features page needs ordered sections with visuals, a bento overview, a benefits recap and a CTA at the end.
Sources: ShantanuKumar ([How to Design Amazing Landing pages w/ Readymag.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dro9e5_VT2U)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Layouts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0Pk3OVbxRU)); TimGabe ([I Redesigned a Neobank App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GMQRCnOQmo)); willpatersondesign ([Graphic Designer Reviews AWESOME Design Work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XrCCQejV03k))

### Close with testimonials, a benefits recap and one CTA
Testimonials sit right before the final ask. Repeat 3-4 main benefits beside the final CTA (a bento beats a plain list), as sales pages do. Many free-trial startups omit pricing from the homepage, so the CTA block may be signup or demo only. Track how many visitors reach it (scroll depth, session recordings).
Sources: DesignSpo ([The Perfect Website Layout For Startups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z7r5RNo-hN0)); DesignSpo ([21 Website Layouts You Should Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZGR72wgOT4))

### Pull the best stat out of the paragraph
A strong number buried in prose ("students improve 200+ points") should be bold, colored, with an arrow, and repeated as a row of 3 big numbers with small labels under the hero. Add a text or icon cue so the color is not the only signal.
Sources: DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E))

### Comparison, FAQ and form pages
Comparison page: be bold, not neutral. Headline with a curiosity claim that names the pain, visuals of how you solve it, one CTA. In the table highlight your own column (background or bolder type), use row dividers, and avoid yellow marks that read as "missing feature". FAQ does not sell: handle objections in the hero and benefit sections, then style the FAQ as an accordion in brand voice, with answers from real support tickets. Contact or signup form: labels written as part of a sentence, one qualifying question when lead quality matters, matched to the site design, with email and address beside it to vet leads.
Sources: DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Layouts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0Pk3OVbxRU)); DesignSpo ([21 Website Layouts You Should Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZGR72wgOT4))

### Add a mission band only if you disrupt something
Works when the product disrupts a broken industry, framed as a goal people relate to ("separate anxiety from money"), with a "read the full manifesto" link. Otherwise skip it.
Sources: DesignSpo ([The Perfect Website Layout For Startups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z7r5RNo-hN0))

### Keep the page light
Speaker cites: a 4-second load loses 78% more visitors than a 1-second load; a study of 4 billion sites found average mobile load over 8 seconds. Target page weight under 2 MB and test on throttled mobile.
Sources: DesignSpo ([Give me 8 minutes and I'll make your website 278% faster](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mXv4NPp5IPM))

## Social proof and trust signals
### Put proof in the first screen, beside the CTA
Logo strip, star rating or testimonial directly under the hero button. Lead with the most recognizable logo; investor proof (Y Combinator) works like a famous school on a resume; young startups can swap early users to earn logos. Compact version: overlapping avatars (`margin-left:-8px`, 2px border), five stars, "5.0" and the review count. Logos in one color, same-height boxes, even spacing. Speaker cites his own research that first-section proof raises conversion.
- Look for: proof only in the footer or far below, claims like "rated number one" with no source.
Sources: DesignSpo ([The Perfect Website Layout For Startups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z7r5RNo-hN0)); DesignSpo ([I redesigned YOUR websites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-F1F4YISY4E)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8Vp8k3jXaA)); TimGabe ([9 SECRETS To Beautiful Landing Page Design Pt. 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vBuyR8uF380))

### Make proof hard to dismiss
Users distrust everything. Prefer audio or phone-shot video over text. Third-party sources (Clutch, Google Reviews) beat anything you control. If none exist, be concrete: "revenue rose 20% after launch" beats "we helped them grow". Testimonials get their own full-width section, a quote headline per item, name, role and a link to the source, grouped by customer type so visitors see people like them. Tie each to a goal and a result. A recognizable name (a chief marketing officer) is worth more than a pile of unknowns. Never fake them.
- Look for: scattered tweets, hard-to-read handwritten notes, a cramped side column.
- Fix: repeat short testimonials near CTAs and the price.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Seismic Shifts Happening In Web Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oY87jJx4sAI)); DesignSpo ([PRO Vs AMATEUR Website Layouts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L0Pk3OVbxRU)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Best Way to Market Your Web Design Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLyf8XvwNgw)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Grow a Strong Web Design Business with SEO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bhR0HaeFwJg))

### Replace claims with verifiable specifics
Show people, dates, exact terms and imperfection: real names and faces, delivery dates, when billing happens, how to cancel. Real photos of real events beat polished stock. Vague virtue words ("natural", "eco-friendly") plus earthy imagery signal virtue without proof; back claims with numbers, certifications or links. Where output is generated, cite sources (Perplexity-style) so users can verify.
Sources: hatchconference ([Designing Products for Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rq9djF2dqI)); SatoriGraphics ([Graphic Designer EXPOSES 'Eco-Friendly' Branding Lies!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eNBSW_tRnfo)); DesignSpo ([$1,000,000 Design Portfolios](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DaeEKKSpgXU))

### Fix doubt at the exact point it occurs
Skyscanner research: users trusted the brand but not unfamiliar partner sellers, so a "Recommended provider" badge scored on reviews and price accuracy was added beside the partner list. Put trust markers next to the unfamiliar party, tied to criteria you can state. Place guarantees (no commitment, pause anytime, money-back) in a visually distinct tile next to plan cards, where anxiety peaks.
Sources: hatchconference ([How Design Moves Revenue, Retention, and Market Share at Skyscanner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5yJYLrbvOYY)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([33 UNIQUE Website Layouts with Real World Project Examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nZ2BJt9PgKE))

### Show real live numbers
Live activity beats a claim: presence counts, reaction counts, recent signups with avatars (Discord shows "2312 online" even to strangers). Show only real numbers.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top 1% Apps Keep You Hooked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ))

### Ship the trust pages and polish the money screens
Before launch: About, Contact, Privacy, Terms, Refund pages, linked from pricing and checkout. For finance, crypto, health and insurance, transitions, loading states and alignment on signup, payment and account pages are trust signals, so spend polish time there first. Consistent patterns read as promises kept; the same action must look and behave the same everywhere.
Sources: ShantanuKumar ([How to build a Clothing Brand in 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L7mOGopsIuY)); TimGabe ([The Secret Behind Weirdly Addictive Apps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du2lkZ_cux8)); hatchconference ([Designing Products for Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rq9djF2dqI))

### Give the visitor the right to try
Undo, a clear cancel path, plain text on what happens next, and a sandbox or test mode for risky features let people click without fear. Track trust as events: permission accept and deny rate, undo and cancel use, return after an error, second-session behavior after a big ask. Treat the number of screens crossed before first value (trust surface) as something to shrink.
Sources: hatchconference ([Designing Products for Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rq9djF2dqI))

### Keep the marketing promise inside the product
A marketing site that said "fun company" followed by a spreadsheet-feeling product drops trust at login. Check that the first post-login screen carries the same personality and polish. Tell one story across name, hero imagery, tone and proof (Teremana cited), and drop elements that tell a different one. Pair the brand mark with a descriptor ("Skincare") so a bare icon or abstract name does not have to explain what you are.
Sources: Hatch Conference ([Building design systems that work for your whole Organisation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ljl0aUdcpg)); SatoriGraphics ([Brand Design's MOST IMPORTANT Technique!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpmtYxaODXk)); willpatersondesign ([Reviewing CLEAN Subscriber DESIGN work Ep 32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EzeqtbjPF3E))

## Pricing pages and paywalls
### Test structure before price
Speaker cites Adapty 2026 data: plan-count changes drive 63% more conversion uplift than price tests; trial structure changes give a 59.6% LTV uplift; localizing language and currency gives 62.3%. Test plan count, plan order, trial framing and localization before changing the number. Speaker cites that apps running 50+ paywall experiments a year earn 18.7x more than apps running one. These figures come from consumer subscription apps; apply to B2B with care.
- Fix: make the paywall configurable and test one structural change every week or two.
Sources: TimGabe ([I Studied 10,000 Paywall Screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI)); TimGabe ([How Top Apps Scientifically Design Paywalls That Convert](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qJ4qYhTqAz0))

### Default layout: three cards, toggle, compare table
Short headline ("Pricing"), one-line subtitle about the main benefit, monthly/annual toggle with a savings label, three structurally identical cards with air between them, one emphasized, a "talk to sales" option for enterprise, a full compare table below. Each card: header, a few check-icon bullets, one button. Make the preferred plan visibly larger or stronger. Real specifics (amount, serving, limits) fill the knowledge gap on product pages.
Sources: DesignSpo ([21 Website Layouts You Should Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EZGR72wgOT4)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([33 UNIQUE Website Layouts with Real World Project Examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nZ2BJt9PgKE)); SatoriGraphics ([Full Course: 3 PROVEN Principles Of Layout Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GT-K0CrrgfU))

### Show only the delta between tiers
Repeating every feature in every tier makes cheapest and priciest look alike. Group features under category headings and, for higher tiers, write "Everything in Basic, plus:" followed by only the new items.
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([The Psychology of a PERFECT Website](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d-IaU9qcDGg))

### State the charge, the date and how to cancel
A paywall is a trust screen. Speaker cites: Blinkist added a front-and-center line on when and for what users are charged, conversion rose 23%, complaints fell 55%, churn did not change. Show a 3-step trial timeline (Today: unlock; Day 5: reminder that the trial ends; Day 7: first charge, cancel anytime), with "No payment due now" and the promised reminder. Blinkist's visual 7-day explanation raised trial sign-ups 23%. Revealing a downside proactively raises trust (transparency bias, per the speaker). Put the total and the cancellation promise on the button: "Reserve 445 euros total" with "Free cancellation before March 26" beneath.
- Fix: header "How your free trial works", CTA "Start my free trial". Move feature bullets below or drop them.
Sources: TimGabe ([I Studied 10,000 Paywall Screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI)); uxpeak ([Top 3 UX/UI Redesigns That Make You Design Like a Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr37ibqXl1U)); TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk))

### Show one exact price, including fees
Ranges are read as the high number (anchoring), and several ranges at once create too many unknowns. Speaker cites: Uber moved from a range to one estimated price and rides per user rose by double digits. Show one total including taxes and usage estimates, and check that it does not change between steps (Skyscanner's total-cost work made extras transparent early). If variable, show a typical price and a note.
Sources: uxpeak ([Top 3 UX/UI Redesigns That Make You Design Like a Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr37ibqXl1U)); TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk)); hatchconference ([How Design Moves Revenue, Retention, and Market Share at Skyscanner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5yJYLrbvOYY))

### Steer the choice with default, anchor and contrast
Lead with the plan you want chosen and put the lesser one behind a toggle or link. Speaker cites: annual first with monthly one tap away raised annual picks from about 60% to 75-80% at the same price. Give the best plan something others lack (a longer trial on annual: 14 days versus 7 on monthly; a free trial on annual only raised conversion 39% in one case). Frame cost against a bigger number: a $50 add-on under a $1,900 product reads as "just 2.6%", and a premium tier shown first makes the middle one reasonable. Crossed-out price with a small "Save 31%" badge anchors a deal; keep the badge smaller than the price.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top Apps Scientifically Design Paywalls That Convert](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qJ4qYhTqAz0)); TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk)); uxpeak ([The UX Psychology Behind Apps People Can't Stop Using](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2TlIg3VokY8)); uxpeak ([Top 3 UX/UI Redesigns That Make You Design Like a Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr37ibqXl1U))

### Keep the first paywall a simple choice
Speaker cites: Mojo hid AI credit packs on its first paywall with no price or copy change, and new subscription revenue grew 14% within a week, because packs turned a simple choice into a comparison. Show only the main subscription options first and move add-ons to later screens. Speaker cites: Slope's one-tap free trial raised trial starts 25%, so cut checkout to one confirmation after the plan is chosen.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top Apps Scientifically Design Paywalls That Convert](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qJ4qYhTqAz0)); TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk))

### Let users earn value before the wall
Speaker cites: Pregnancy+ tested a discounted intro price against a no-payment free trial and the trial earned 140% more revenue (endowment effect). Do not blur the user's own content behind a paywall; ask a qualifying question (sessions per month) and recommend a plan. Speaker cites: Busuu limited free users to one language, casual users were unaffected and engaged users, at peak intent, converted 43% more. Gate on usage milestones (second project, Nth export), not at signup. Show the paywall after the first felt win: speaker cites 82% of subscription starts happen on install day (RevenueCat; Adapty says 89.4%). Soft limits and previews before a hard wall; free should not feel punishing.
Sources: TimGabe ([How Top Apps Scientifically Design Paywalls That Convert](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qJ4qYhTqAz0)); TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk)); TimGabe ([I Studied 10,000 Paywall Screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI)); TimGabe ([How a Chinese Unicorn App 'Out-Addicts' Netflix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dCZrtH15zc))

### Show what is locked
Speaker cites: Pro labels on existing premium content in a journaling product raised free-to-paid conversion 35% (loss aversion). Put a small Pro badge in lists and menus and explain on click. Upgrade prompts land where a premium feature solves the visible problem, not on login or random modals. Block drip-locks and forced waits unless the user asked for pacing (Blinkist's "one piece per day" test drew strong negative signals).
Sources: TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk)); TimGabe ([How Tinder's Design Hacked Society](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA)); Hatch Conference ([Tarek Sadi, Designing for your Product Vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CBpzj5MJBUY))

### Keep credits and spending visible
Variable prices, awkward coin bundles, no spend dashboard and rising prices hide real cost and exploit sunk cost; the speaker calls it a dark pattern. Show balance, cost per action in credits and money, usage to date, and bundles that map to round prices. Offer a "hide amounts" toggle where balances are sensitive. Keep rewards in one calm place with line-item history, not banners on every screen.
Sources: TimGabe ([How a Chinese Unicorn App 'Out-Addicts' Netflix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dCZrtH15zc)); TimGabe ([I Redesigned a Neobank App](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GMQRCnOQmo))

### Drop fake urgency
"Only 1 left at this price", countdowns and limited-time badges trigger fear buying. Ask whether the user needs the fact to get value or whether it only pushes a fast purchase. Keep only real, useful constraints. Soften pushy copy and alarm colors with a refund line or security note, and add space around the CTA.
Sources: hatchconference ([The Danger of Gaming the System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_m2T86qycPA)); SatoriGraphics ([The ONLY Video You Need To Learn High-Level Design Thinking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzQl1Tl_LPM))

### Weekly plans and higher prices (consumer apps only)
Speaker cites RevenueCat 2026: median conversion 2.8% for high-priced apps versus 2% for mid-priced, top quartile 6.1%; weekly plans grew from 43% to 55.6% of subscription-app revenue and convert 1.7-7.4x better than annual; weekly with a trial gave $54.50 12-month LTV versus $7.40 without. Do not default to the cheapest tier. Use with care for B2B SaaS.
Sources: TimGabe ([I Studied 10,000 Paywall Screens](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI))

### Benchmark context
Speaker cites: the average SaaS site converts under 4% of visitors; the best near 12%. Context for goals, not a precise target.
Sources: TimGabe ([Give Me 10 Minutes & I'll Boost Your Website Conversions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs))

## Microcopy inside products
### Fewest words, matched to the action
Four words for "rewards" is too many. A button about "earning tokens" when the action is claiming voting rewards is wrong; "Commit time" reads as committing time, not votes. Every label is verb plus object that matches what happens next. Cut words the context already says (header "Voting", so the list needs no "Last 10 votes"); across a full app this removes real clutter. Run a dedicated copy pass at the end on buttons, errors and labels.
- Look for: repeated nouns in a heading and its labels; a label whose result is a different thing.
Sources: TimGabe ([4 levels of UI/UX design (and BIG mistakes to avoid)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86PGRyQjdzQ)); amirmoradiUX ([The 14 UX Deliverables That Get You Hired](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xyyMVYMOO6Y))

### Replace adjectives with numbers and dates
"Specificity is trust." "Delivery in 23 minutes" beats "fast delivery". "2 minutes away" turns a cost decision into a convenience one. "Friday, March 28 to Wednesday, April 2" with a "5 nights" badge beats two date fields. Show computed totals so users do no math. Titles: one emotional hook plus one concrete detail ("Beachside escape steps from the sand").
Sources: uxpeak ([Top 3 UX/UI Redesigns That Make You Design Like a Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zr37ibqXl1U)); uxpeak ([$42K vs $297K UX/UI Design: The Difference Is HUGE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7jTCgIWS4w))

### Put real counts in "see all" buttons
"View all 128 templates", "See all 42 integrations", with the live count. It sets expectations and sparks curiosity.
Sources: uxpeak ([UX/UI Design Tutorial in Figma - Design Modern Website from Scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SIoOS6tFw1Y))

### Pick words by the feeling you want
"Trial" feels transactional; "guest pass" feels like an invitation. Speaker cites: Headspace's "guest pass" (30 days) lifted sign-ups 7%. Netflix uses conversational category names. Review button and plan labels for transactional wording and test a warmer alternative.
Sources: TimGabe ([Copy These SaaS Growth Tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk)); TimGabe ([How Netflix Made Us All Binge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hIm3d3JAsPw))

### Write lapsed and missed states as recovery
"Pause your streak, pick up right where you left off" and "vacation mode, up to 14 days" frame a miss as fine. Missed credit can move to when the user finishes rather than vanish. No shame copy on streak-break or lapsed-user states.
Sources: TimGabe ([Retention 101: How Top 1% Apps Keep Users for Years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4))

### Count "you" against "we"
More you/your than I/we/our keeps the customer as the hero. Grep landing and onboarding copy and rewrite feature statements as what the user can now do. Also grep for gendered defaults (chairman, guys, he/she) and use neutral words with "you".
Sources: SelfMadeWebDesigner ([how to STAND OUT AS A FREELANCER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZqAh0KzfSn8)); hatchconference ([Luke Murphy: Systems of Exclusion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLtVIEcHdSo))

### Use format placeholders and past-behavior prompts
An email field showing "jane@doe.com" signals the input (placeholder shows format, never replaces the label). In feedback prompts ask "tell me about the last time you..." not "would you like...", since opinions are unreliable and wording and order change answers.
Sources: TimGabe ([Steal This Landing Page Hero Workflow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA)); SelfMadeWebDesigner ([How to Build a UX Design Portfolio that Get's You Hired w/ Sarah Doody](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDPZOqMoS9g))

### Use an LLM to lint voice, not to write it
Keep a voice and tone note in the repo. After writing copy, have a model scan for inconsistent terms, jargon and tone shifts, then rewrite by hand where it sounds generic. Panelists say models are not great writers; human time goes to concepts like how "knowledge", "memory" and "context" differ.
Sources: Panel ([AI is Redesigning Design Tools - with Lovable, V0 and Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rrt_MDrpraU))

### Mind outreach and email too
A cold email from a new anonymous address asking for a file download was treated as spam and never opened. Use a recognizable sender, put the value inline and avoid unexplained download links.
Sources: willpatersondesign ([I Secretly Redesigned Local Company Logos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mjtSEWPJIAs))

## Where designers disagree
### Primary button shape and CTA pairs
Notes suggest contrast by shape (a rounded button on a square layout) and a 48px gap between two hero CTAs. frontend-design bans rounded-full pills on primary buttons and wants one filled action per view with neutral peers. Follow frontend-design for shape; the shared point is that the primary must differ clearly from its secondary.

### Looping logo marquee
One tutorial uses a slow looping logo marquee (duplicated strip, about 12s per loop). frontend-design bans loops of micro-animations in the hero and requires pausing off-screen loops. Prefer a static strip; if a marquee is used, pause off-screen and honor reduced motion.

### Discount badges and crossed-out prices
uxpeak uses a crossed-out price with a green "-31%" badge and says to keep the badge small. Will Paterson claims discounting lowers perceived value (his experience, no study) and prefers full price or free, with a free tier or time-limited trial instead of coupons. Use a savings label on annual only, small, and avoid stacking constant discount badges.

### Loss-framed upgrade prompts
uxpeak recommends naming the files the user will lose with a countdown and a dismiss reading "I'll risk it" (Kahneman: loss is about twice as strong as gain). That pushes against frontend-design's no-blame copy and the notes' own warning on dark urgency. If used, state only true losses, sparingly, and keep dismiss neutral.

### CTA count
Unbounce data as cited (1 CTA 3.5%, 2 CTAs 11.9%, 3 CTAs 10.5%) sits beside the near-universal advice to use one. Reconcile as one primary plus one quiet secondary, never three or more.

### Pop-ups and gated freebies
One speaker reports success with a lead-magnet pop-up while noting some dislike them; others argue for free value first and no forced waits. If used, trigger on intent, not on load.

### Beautiful versus converting
Several designers argue clarity and conversion beat polish; Tim Gabe and others also say polish itself is a trust signal in money and health products. Both hold: polish serves clarity, and a polished page that weakens the headline or CTA loses.
